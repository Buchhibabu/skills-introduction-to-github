# Draft v1 (lead editor) — "The Flywheel"

COLD OPEN
- It costs billions of dollars a year to run.
- Nine hundred million people use it every week.
- The vast majority pay nothing.
- Have you ever asked why?
[TITLE: THE FLYWHEEL]

EXHIBIT A — THE LAUNCH
- November thirtieth, twenty twenty-two. OpenAI releases a free research preview called ChatGPT.
- Experts called it a toy. Stack Overflow banned its answers within a week.
- The public didn't care. One million users in five days. An estimated hundred million in two months.
- And why was it free? It's in the launch post: "to get users' feedback."
- Now here's what almost nobody noticed.
- GPT-4 had already finished training. August twenty twenty-two. Three months before ChatGPT launched.
- Scale built the raw model. More data, more compute, predictably better. That's the scaling law.
- But when GPT-4 shipped, OpenAI said it had been improved with "feedback submitted by ChatGPT users."

EXHIBIT B — THE WHEEL
- That's the flywheel.
- You use it. Your prompts, your corrections, your thumbs-up become signal. The model improves. More people come. The wheel spins faster.
- In twenty twenty-five, OpenAI admitted it had turned thumbs-up and thumbs-down data into a reward signal. The model became such a flatterer, they rolled it back.
- A Stanford review found all six major American AI developers train on users' chats by default.

EXHIBIT C — THE GRAVEYARD
- Meanwhile, a gold rush. Thousands of startups wrapped GPT-4 in a clean interface and raised money.
- Then, November twenty twenty-three. One keynote: custom GPTs, built-in document search, bigger memory. Whole product categories became a feature.
- Sam Altman had said it out loud: build as if the models won't improve, and "we're going to steamroll you."
- Every new model spawns a wave of startups. The next one can erase them.

EXHIBIT D — THE ACCELERATION
- Then the wheel reached the coders. "Reflexive AI usage is now a baseline expectation," Shopify's CEO told staff. Across the industry, the message was the same: use it.
- In late twenty twenty-three, the best model could fix about two percent of real GitHub issues in a benchmark. By February twenty twenty-six, Claude Opus four point six reached about eighty.
- This year, GitHub began training on Copilot interactions from individual users, unless they opt out.

EXHIBIT E — THE PRICE
- So why is it nearly free?
- Investor money pays part of it. Ads are starting to pay part of it. And part of the price is paid in data. Yours.
- You can switch it off. Most people never open the settings.
- To be fair, it's a trade, and often a good one. You get a better tool. They get a better model.
- But if your plan is a thin layer on someone else's model, understand: the wheel keeps turning, and the next release won't ask permission.

THE ONE ASSET
- So build. Raise. Ship. Ride the wheel.
- But invest in the one asset no model release can take from you.
- Your attention. On screens, it now lasts about forty-seven seconds.
- Your mind. In Anthropic's own study, coders who leaned on AI scored seventeen percent lower on what they had just learned.
- Your body. People who exercise report about forty percent fewer bad mental-health days.
- The models will keep getting better. Make sure you do too.
- Invest consciously.

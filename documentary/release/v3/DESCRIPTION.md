# THE FLYWHEEL — a case in five exhibits (v3)

Why is ChatGPT, which OpenAI says more than a billion people use every week, nearly free? This documentary builds the case one exhibit at a time from public documents: the launch post, system cards, postmortems, memos and policy pages. It shows how your clicks, chats and code help train the next model, and who gets steamrolled when the wheel turns. It ends on the one asset no model release can upgrade for you. Runtime 4:08.

## How this film was made
- All voices, music and sound effects are AI-generated with ElevenLabs. The narrator is an original designed voice. No real person's voice was imitated or cloned.
- No voice is filtered, processed or layered: every voice is a single, natural performance.
- The 3D footage (the city, server halls, the flywheel, the people made of light, the dawn) is computer-generated illustration built in code, not real footage of real places or people.
- Quotes are shown on screen and read by the narrator (an AI voice), not by the people or companies quoted.
- The users' voices and prompts, the founder pitch, the startup categories and the settings screen are illustrative, and labelled as such.

## Fairness notes
- Opting out is possible. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and GitHub Copilot all have settings that stop consumer chats from training models, and business/API plans are excluded by default. Most people never change defaults.
- Data is one part of why AI is cheap, not the only one. Investor money, a race for users, ads and falling costs matter too.
- "Now a feature" is not "shut down". Thin wrappers felt the squeeze most; many companies built on frontier models survived, were squeezed, or were bought.
- The Microsoft memo is known only from a reported leak. Shopify's memo called AI use "a baseline expectation".
- Benchmark numbers are lab-reported, on different test versions; OpenAI later called SWE-bench Verified contaminated.

# Trailer research bible

Six researchers studied trailer craft, each from one angle: story structure, editing rhythm, attention retention, aesthetics, camera language and sound. Treat their numbers as defaults, not laws.

## STRUCTURE: Story architecture for a 2:15 tech launch trailer with no voiceover: cold open, then three acts, then title drop and button. The film argues its case as a curiosity-driven story told through images, music and text cards that act as dialogue. Every beat is locked to a 120 BPM grid at 30 fps (beat = 1

### Principles
- A trailer has five parts: cold open, Act 1 (introduction), Act 2 (escalation), Act 3 (climax), button. This is Derek Lieu's model. Viewers split trailers into exposition, complication and resolution at roughly 1/4, 1/2, 1/4 of runtime (Iricinschi & Hu, Cornell, CogSci 2014). In a launch trailer the title drop sits between Act 3 and the button.
- Show the energy first. The single most spectacular image in the film appears in the first 2 s as a flash-forward. Meaning is held back, the spectacle is not. In Sony's experience, 3–5 s pre-trailer 'bumpers' raised retention and interest about 4x on mobile.
- Frame 0 is a fully composed, lit image with a sound transient on the same frame. No logo, no fade from black, no ambient pad first. Use at least 2 shots before 0:05 (Google ABCDs: get to the heart of the story faster, with tight framing and engaging pacing). First readable text by 0:02–0:03.
- Cut to the music; never fit music to a finished edit (Neil Oseman). Lock the tempo map before building any visual. Put story beats on 4-bar phrase boundaries (every 8 s at 120 BPM) and cuts/cards on beats or 8ths.
- Curiosity drives retention. Always keep at least one open question (loop) from 0:02 until the title. Open the next loop before closing the current one. Hold back the product's NAME, never the energy. Information-gap theory (Loewenstein, 1994): curiosity peaks when the gap feels small and closable, so ask questions with partial information, not total mystery.
- Link beats with BUT and THEREFORE, never AND THEN (Parker & Stone). A list of capabilities is not a story. If a card can be deleted without breaking the causal chain, delete it.
- Contrast creates energy. Nothing feels loud unless something quiet came before it. The quiet moments here are short and surgical: 0.5–1.0 s of true silence, never a slow passage.
- Escalate every 8-second phrase on at least one axis: scale (powers of ten), cut rate, hit density, camera speed, stakes or proximity. Never plateau for more than 8 s. Insert a planned twist about every 25–30 s (here at 0:32, 0:56, 1:28, 1:58).
- Vary shot lengths; don't just shorten them. In a study of 725 trailers, variance in shot length predicted whether viewers liked or disliked the trailer. Fast bursts need 45–75 f 'breath' shots between them.
- Rule of three: establish, confirm, then break or escalate. Three parallel beats, each bigger. The 4th beat breaks the pattern.
- Without VO, text cards ARE the dialogue. Give the typography distinct 'voices' and write the cards as call-and-response. The best text is diegetic: text the world itself would type or show (terminal, PR titles, Slack, budget rows).
- Visuals carry most of the meaning. Keep readable narrator text on screen for no more than ~35–40% of runtime, and at least 60% of the runtime should be pure picture. Composite cards into the 3D space rather than placing them on black.
- A trailer sells a world and a feeling, not a plot summary (Mark Woollen). Sell the software factory as a place the viewer wants to stand in, not a feature list.
- Tech launch trailers differ from movie trailers in several ways. (a) The antagonist is abstract (friction, backlog, time), so it must be made physical (a ticket tower, a dark production line). (b) The product IS the resolution, so withhold the title, not the capability. (c) Proof replaces money shots, and must be literal and verifiable because technical buyers punish hype. (d) A call to action replaces the release-date card. (e) Brand precision (Apple, Linear: one idea per shot, monochrome plus one accent) is fused with movie-trailer architecture and sound energy.
- Music changes mood at only 2–3 planned points (Oseman). The biggest change is preceded by a music stop: 'black and silent except one line, then BOOM'.
- Use the BRAAM ('preview pulse', popularized by the Inception trailer) at structural joints only, 3 times at most in 2:15.
- Always end with a button: a short beat after the title (callback, wink or hook) so the last memory is energy, not a logo (Josh Stallings; filmeditingpro).
- Time is the trailer's biggest enemy (Woollen). Every second must buy either information or emotion. Hollywood trailers average about 2:03 (Stephen Follows, 6,926 trailers), so 2:15 has to earn its extra 12 s.

### Techniques
- **Flash-forward cold open (in-media-res bumper)**. *When:* 0:00–0:08 (bars 1–4). Always needed here: the brief says the energy must be visible first and v1 was too slow.
  *How:* 1) Take the 3–4 most spectacular images from the Act 3 factory and show them out of context. f0–14: macro of a single amber light streaking along a rail. f15–44: TOP-DOWN crane-up revealing ~1,000 rails lighting in a cascade (expo.in). f30–44 can split into an FPV skim. f45–59: WORM'S-EYE of a colossal structure with a light sweep travelling upward. 2) A suckback ends exactly at f60. 3) f60: BRAAM #1, 2-frame white flash, first narrator card slammed in 3D space. 4) f120 and f150: a split card on beats 1 and 3. 5) f180–239: 'rewind'. Lights extinguish in reverse cascade over 45 f, camera yanks back (expo.in), tape-stop pitches down 1 octave over 0.7 s, single tick at f225. 6) Hard-cut into Act 1 at f240. Total ≤8 s: the Joker teaser's ~9 s cold open is the upper bound for a tense piece. Hobbs & Shaw's ~45 s cold open works only for comedy. Don't make the bumper a verbatim replay of later shots; Woollen calls that 'self-cannibalism'. The same shots come back recontextualized in Act 3 (see Bookend).
  *Reference:* Studio bumpers (Slashfilm: Sony's Elias Plishner reports ~4x retention and interest on mobile). Derek Lieu's Noita and Tactical Breach Wizards trailers open already in motion. Steal: start mid-action, show the 'end state' first, explain nothing.
- **Frame-0 / first-3-seconds rule**. *When:* First frame and first 3 s of every cut, including 60 s and 30 s cutdowns.
  *How:* Frame 0 is a composed, high-contrast still that works as the autoplay thumbnail. The audio transient lands at f0, with no fade-in. At least 2 cuts by 0:05 (the template has 4 by 0:02). Readable text by f60–90. Within the first 3 s: no logo, no black hold longer than 6 f, no slow pan. Keep tight framing: subject at least 30% of frame height in the first two shots.
  *Reference:* Google ABCDs (≥2 shots in first 5 s, tight framing). A trailer editor's AMA (Dark Horizons): a flashy 3-second open retains viewers far better than green-band cards plus studio logos.
- **Open-loop ledger**. *When:* Script stage, then re-checked at every edit.
  *How:* Keep a table with columns id, the viewer's question, opened_at, closed_at. Rules: (1) at least 1 loop open from 0:02 to the title; (2) no more than 2 unresolved at once (no VO means less working memory); (3) open loop N+1 at least 2 s before closing loop N; (4) close loops with an image where possible, not a card; (5) the button deliberately opens one final loop, which serves as the call to action. Ledger for the template: L1 'what is this place / what is the next wave?' opens 0:02 and closes 1:48 (bookend) plus 1:58 (title). L2 'can the backlog be beaten?' opens 0:16, falsely closes 0:48, reopens 0:56 and closes 1:20. L3 'who runs the line?' opens 1:29 and closes 1:32–1:58. L4 'the clock is running' opens 2:10 and stays open.
  *Reference:* Information-gap theory (Loewenstein 1994). Lieu's breakdowns on which questions to raise and leave unanswered.
- **Tricolon crescendo (rule of three)**. *When:* Problem statement (0:16–0:22), station ignition and fleet volley (1:22–1:25), and the final pre-title volley (1:52–1:54).
  *How:* Place three syntactically parallel cards of 3 words or fewer on consecutive downbeats: bars (2 s apart) in Acts 1–2, beats or 1 s apart in Act 3. Each step is bigger on at least 2 axes: type scale +15–25%, one extra hit layer (low tom → +metal → +sub/braam), a more extreme camera angle, and 10x scene scale. Beat 4 breaks the pattern: the tower topples toward the camera, a Slack flood arrives, or silence falls.
  *Reference:* Rhetorical tricolon. In the template: 'TICKETS PILE UP. / REVIEWS WAIT. / RELEASES SLIP.', then the backlog tower collapses.
- **Refrain-and-flip turn**. *When:* The turn from problem to solution, or the tagline.
  *How:* Repeat one line in the same font, position, sound and screen location 2–3 times over escalating problem imagery. On the turn frame, change ONE word and flip the palette from cold blue-gray to warm amber on that exact frame, with a hit. Example: 'THE LINE WAITS.' x2, then 'THE LINE RUNS.'
  *Reference:* Anthropic 'Keep thinking' (Sept 2025, 90 s hero film, Mother / Love Song / dir. Daniel Wolfe). It repeats a 'never been a worse time' refrain over a problem montage, then flips it to 'better time'. Steal both the device and the brand tone: AI that amplifies problem-solvers.
- **Text cards as dialogue: three typographic voices**. *When:* Wherever text carries story. Since there is no VO, that is everywhere.
  *How:* V1 WORLD: lowercase mono, cap height 3.5–4.5% of frame, typed at 1–2 chars per frame, cursor blinking every 15 f (phase-locked to 120 BPM), shown as diegetic UI inside the scene. V2 NARRATOR: condensed caps, cap height 7–12%, slammed on hits. The slam is scale 1.12→1.00 over 5 f (expo.out), opacity 0→1 in 1–2 f, tracking +2%→0, and an 8 px camera shake decaying with τ≈4 f. V3 PRODUCT: warm accent, speaks only after the reveal and answers V1's questions. Write in call-and-response: V1 asks or complains on beat 1, V2 answers on beat 3. Hold in frames = 12 + 8 × words. Single primed words in a rhythmic run can drop to 8 f; never below 6 f. At most 7 words per card. Budget about 120 words and about 40 cards for the whole 2:15. Split sentences across beats ('THE NEXT WAVE' / 'ISN'T AN AGENT.' / 'IT'S THE LINE.'). Cut cards out on the next beat in Acts 2–3, never fade them.
  *Reference:* The Social Network trailer (2010, Mark Woollen & Associates) builds its world for about 50 s through Facebook-style product imagery before any film footage, under one choir track with a 'gentle intro → build → swell → land' arc.
- **Diegetic screen-life storytelling**. *When:* Act 1's problem (0:22–0:24 Slack flood) and Act 2A's 5-month timeline (0:32–0:48).
  *How:* Each plot point is an artifact a character would actually type or see: terminal output, a PR title, a Slack ping, a calendar invite, a budget-spreadsheet row. Render the artifacts as floating panes in 3D (depth-sorted, DOF) and fly the camera between them. Use 6–12 artifacts per 16 s, each legible for at least 0.8 s; most are texture and only the KEY line must be read. The key artifact (the 'APPROVED' budget row) gets a hit and a push-in. The viewer infers what connects them, and that participation is the hook.
  *Reference:* Google 'Parisian Love' (Super Bowl 2010, ~52 s). An entire romance told through typed search queries, from 'study abroad paris france' to 'how to assemble a crib', with end card 'Search on.' Also the opening desktop montage of Searching (2018). Steal: compress 5 months into about 16 s of UI.
- **Data as event (one number, one comparison, physical units)**. *When:* The 5-month 'capability → budget' story, agent counts, and the proof volley.
  *How:* Rules: one number per beat; every number has from→to and a felt unit (months, agents, stations). Counters roll over 24–36 f with power4.out, and the last digit lands 1 f before the hit. Make units physical. Months are 3D gates the camera flies through, at accelerating intervals of 8 s → 6 s → 2 s, so acceleration is felt rather than stated. Growth is a column or swarm the camera must crane up to follow until it leaves frame, then a pull-back reveals the scale. Never show a static chart with more than 3 bars, and never two numbers on one card in bam-bam sections.
  *Reference:* Neil Halloran, 'The Fallen of World War II' (2015): the camera rides a rising bar off-screen until the scale becomes visceral. Steal that move for agent counts and the budget line.
- **Clock spine (time as the antagonist)**. *When:* The whole film. This is the throughline for the client's '5 months' argument.
  *How:* A tick on every beat (every 15 f at 120 BPM) runs under Act 1. It survives every music drop as the only sound left, becomes the month counter in Act 2A, and returns in the button (MONTH 0 → MONTH 1). Sync all visual blinks (cursor, factory lamps, station pulses) to the tick so picture and sound share one pulse. Run a Shepard-tone riser under the 8 s before the drop (1:20–1:28) for endless-feeling tension.
  *Reference:* Dunkirk trailers (2017): ticking pocket watch plus Shepard tone. SpaceX countdowns. Steal: let the clock be the villain, so the 5-month figure reads as a threat, not trivia.
- **Recontextualizing pull-back (top-angle midpoint reversal)**. *When:* Midpoint, ~41–47% (0:56–1:04).
  *How:* Start tight on the thing the audience believes is the answer: the blazing 'code' station. Music STOPS on the 0:56 downbeat, leaving tick plus drone. Then one continuous 4.0 s move (f1680–1799): dolly back, crane up to TOP-DOWN, ease in-out cubic, FOV narrowing slightly for a reverse dolly-zoom feel. It reveals the station is one small lit box in a long line of DARK stations: PLAN · DESIGN · REVIEW · TEST · SECURE · DEPLOY · OPERATE. A card lands as the move settles ('BUT CODE WAS ONE STATION.'). This is the only shot longer than 3 s outside the title.
  *Reference:* Powers of Ten (Eames, 1977) logic. Classic trailer reveal-by-pullback. It also delivers the 'top angle' the client asked for, with narrative meaning: the god's-eye view is the moment of understanding.
- **Powers-of-ten escalation ladder**. *When:* From Act 2A through Act 3.
  *How:* Each phrase multiplies the visible agent lights by 10: 1 → 10 → 100 → 1,000 → 10,000 → 100,000, with a corner counter if helpful. Each phrase also steps a second axis. Average shot length halves every 16 s in Act 2C (60 f → 30 f → 15 f → 8 f). Hit density doubles (every bar → every 2 beats → every beat → 8ths). Camera speed rises about 1.5x per phrase. Never repeat a scale.
  *Reference:* Mad Max: Fury Road trailers (2014–15): relentless step-ups with no plateau.
- **Music-first tempo map with a single-source cue list**. *When:* Before any visual work, and as the backbone of the render pipeline.
  *How:* 120 BPM in 4/4 at 30 fps gives beat = 15 f, bar = 60 f, phrase = 240 f, and 8ths alternating 7/8 f. Other frame-integer tempos: 150 BPM (12 f per beat, 16ths = 3 f), 100 (18 f), 75 (24 f). Write the trailer as ONE JSON cue list ({bar, beat, sub, type, payload}) that drives both the numpy audio render and the GSAP/Three.js timeline: frame = (bar−1)×60 + (beat−1)×15 + round(sub×15). Lengthen or shorten music only at bar boundaries. Place cuts 0–1 f BEFORE the audio transient, never after.
  *Reference:* Neil Oseman: 'ALWAYS CUT YOUR TRAILER TO MUSIC.' No Film School: mark every beat and color every 4th bar.
- **Three mood changes with music stops**. *When:* Act joints: 0:32, 1:04, 1:32.
  *How:* Exactly 3 musical states change. 0:32: drone+tick → pulse ostinato. 1:04: → full hybrid percussion. 1:32: → climax theme (widest stereo, choir/brass-like pads). Each is preceded by a stop on a downbeat: a 0.5 s gap at 0:30, 8 s of tick-only from 0:56, and 1.0 s of digital silence at 1:28. SFX vocabulary: hit (accent), suckback (ends EXACTLY on the cut frame), whoosh (bridge), riser (2–8 bars, must resolve on a hit or a cut to silence), drone (bed). BRAAMs only at 0:02, 1:32 and 1:58.
  *Reference:* Oseman: montage, then black and silent except one key line, then BOOM, new music. Jessica Fox (Mark Woollen & Assoc.) on the Inception 'preview pulse'.
- **The drop (false ending / dark moment)**. *When:* ~65% of runtime (1:28–1:32), immediately before the climax.
  *How:* Hard cut to pure black on the last 16th of the loudest section so far (the end of the Shepard riser). Digital silence for 30 f, not a fade. At 1:29.0, a single tick. A V1 lowercase line types at 1 char per 2 f ('who runs the line?', ~36 f) with the cursor blinking on the tick. Two sub thumps 15 f apart at 1:30.5 and 1:31.0. A 0.75 s suckback ends exactly at 1:32.0, where the climax hits.
  *Reference:* Oseman's 'black and silent except one line'. The silence-at-the-peak device used heavily in Nolan-era trailers such as Oppenheimer.
- **Shot-length schedule with deliberate variance**. *When:* The whole edit.
  *How:* Target ASL by section: cold open 0.5–0.7 s; Act 1 1.5–2.0 s; Act 2A 1.2 → 0.75 s; midpoint one 4 s shot; Act 2C 1.0 → 0.25 s; Act 3 about 0.5 s, with flutter bursts of 4–6 f for at most 1 bar; title hold 4 s. After every 6–10 fast cuts, insert a 45–75 f breath shot. That gives about 110–130 cuts in 2:15 (ASL about 1.05–1.2 s). For calibration, the Bourne Ultimatum trailer runs about 0.78 s per shot. No 2-second window may pass without a change (cut, card, camera acceleration or sound event).
  *Reference:* IEEE study of 725 trailers: shot-length variance predicts like/dislike. Robservatory cut counts for the Bourne Ultimatum trailer.
- **Angle grammar and screen direction per act**. *When:* Shot planning. This answers the client's 'top angle / bottom angle' ask with narrative meaning.
  *How:* Act 1 (problem): WORM'S-EYE at the problem (camera 0.3–1 m, ~20 mm-equivalent FOV, looking up 25–40°), Dutch tilt 5–12°, protagonist light under 5% of frame, cold palette, Perlin handheld 0.3–0.6°. Act 2A (progress): lateral tracking, with progress always moving LEFT→RIGHT. Midpoint: TOP-DOWN god's-eye, the system view of understanding. Act 2C: FPV fly-throughs along the line, alternating with top-down. Act 3: LOW-ANGLE HERO on the reveal, slow orbit at 15–30°/s, perfect symmetry for order and control. Button: eye-level, static and calm. Threats move toward the camera; solutions expand outward.
  *Reference:* Dune (2020 trailer): tiny figures against colossal structures, under a choir cover of Pink Floyd's 'Eclipse'. Steal: monumentality makes 'the factory' feel epic.
- **Center-weighted framing for fast cuts**. *When:* Any run of shots shorter than 12 f (Act 2C end, Act 3 volleys, the pre-title flutter).
  *How:* Keep the subject and the card in the central ~20% of frame on consecutive fast shots so the eye never searches. Vary scale, angle and color, not screen position. Allow off-center compositions only on shots of 30 f or more.
  *Reference:* Mad Max: Fury Road, whose center framing editor Vashi Nedomansky documented as the reason its hyper-fast cutting stays readable.
- **Partial reveal with a light sweep (Apple grammar)**. *When:* The Act 3 reveal (1:32–1:37) and the title.
  *How:* Start in darkness. A rim or specular light traverses the edge of the mark or structure over 20–30 f. Then 3 macro fragments of 30 f each (1:32 / 1:33 / 1:34), then a full LOW-ANGLE hero shot held 60–90 f with a slow 2–3% push. One idea per shot. Sound is sparse: a swell plus one hit, so the climax theme can breathe. Then switch straight to bam-bam.
  *Reference:* Apple product introduction films (e.g., the iPhone X introduction in 2017, the Apple silicon and Vision Pro introductions): darkness, light sweeps, macro partials before the full object, sparse SFX. Steal the reveal choreography only. Don't copy Apple assets.
- **Light as percussion**. *When:* Every hit from Act 2C on; also the button.
  *How:* Drive emissive intensity, bloom strength and the lamp state of the factory directly from the synthesized audio envelope: attack within 1 f, decay τ≈6–10 f. Each station pulses on its beat. On the title BRAAM, the whole scene's exposure blooms +1.5 EV and decays over 20 f.
  *Reference:* Nothing Phone (1) teasers (2022): Glyph light patterns pulsing in sync with sound in darkness.
- **Bookend recontextualization**. *When:* Act 3, last phrase (1:48–1:52). This closes loop L1.
  *How:* Replay the cold-open shots with identical framing and frame-for-frame timing, changing ONE variable: the palette goes cold → warm, and the amber light is now known to be Claude, the same intelligence at every station. The viewer gets an 'it all connects' payoff without any text.
  *Reference:* The standard flash-forward payoff used in film and game trailers.
- **Title-drop recipe**. *When:* ~87% of runtime (1:58). The single most important frame.
  *How:* Final tricolon at 1:52.0 / 1:53.0 / 1:54.0 with escalating hits. Flash-safe flutter montage of the film's best shots from 1:54 to 1:57, with a riser and snare roll. HARD CUT to black at 1:57.0 (f3510), where the suckback ends. 30 f of digital silence. At 1:58.0 (f3540, the bar-60 downbeat) the title appears ON the hit (BRAAM #3 plus sub drop), not before it. Title motion is minimal: a light sweep across the letterforms (24 f) and a 2–3% push over 4 s. Hold 4 s, the longest hold in the film, with nothing else on screen. Tagline of 6 words or fewer at 2:02 for 2–3 s.
  *Reference:* Classic studio trailer grammar: a hit followed by total quiet, and a sub drop felt under the title.
- **Button recipe**. *When:* After the title, 93–98% of runtime (2:06–2:12).
  *How:* 4–6 s, one idea, one final hit, then cut to black. Three types: callback, wink/punchline, sequel-hook. For this film, combine callback and hook. Black screen; V1 types 'the last wave took 5 months.' (2 s). The counter 'MONTH 0' appears at 2:08.5, then flips to 'MONTH 1' on the 2:10 downbeat with the cold-open tick, a final hit and a 2 f flash. Cut at 2:11. End card (call to action, date, URL) runs 2:12–2:15 while the music tail decays.
  *Reference:* Josh Stallings (Pirates of the Caribbean, Harry Potter trailers) always used a button. Filmeditingpro defines it as 'a final joke, action flourish, or powerful line after the main title.'
- **Story Spine + But/Therefore card script**. *When:* Writing the copy, before any animation.
  *How:* Write the argument as a Story Spine and test every link. Every company runs on software. BUT software still runs on people. THEN agents learned to code. THEREFORE in 5 months a demo became a budget. BUT code was one station. THEREFORE the next wave is the whole line. UNTIL: who runs the line? ANSWER: Claude Opus 5.5. Every card must attach to the previous one with but or therefore; delete any card that attaches with 'and'.
  *Reference:* Kenn Adams' Story Spine. Trey Parker and Matt Stone's 'but/therefore' rule.
- **Watch tests: eyes-closed, muted, pause, 2-second**. *When:* Animatic, then every cut.
  *How:* (1) Eyes closed: the turns, drop and title must be audible from the music alone. (2) Muted at phone size: the story must read from images and type alone; key cards need a cap height of at least 7% of frame. (3) Pause on 10 random frames: every one must be a striking still. (4) Scan for any 2 s window with no change and any shot over 3 s that isn't sanctioned; fix both.
  *Reference:* Josh Stallings had students listen to his Crash trailer with their eyes closed before watching it. Social cuts must work on mute.

=== LAUNCH-TRAILER vs MOVIE-TRAILER DELTAS ===
1) Withhold the NAME, show the capability.
2) Make the abstract antagonist (time, backlog, handoffs) physical.
3) Proof beats replace money shots and must be literal; never fake demos.
4) A call to action replaces the date card.
5) Brand the light and color from frame 0, and say the name at the title. If an ad placement needs the brand in the first 5 s (ABCD), make a separate cut.
6) Brand-grade precision (one idea per shot, monochrome plus one accent) inside trailer architecture.

### Pitfalls
- Slow start (the v1 failure). Logo first, fade-in from black, ambient pad, or a slow establishing pan in the first 3 s. Frame 0 must be a striking image with a sound transient on f0.
- Dead air: any 2 s window with no change (cut, card, camera acceleration or sound event), or any shot longer than 3 s other than the three sanctioned holds: the 4 s midpoint pull-back, the ≤3 s hero orbit and the 4 s title.
- Uniform rhythm: every cut at the same length (e.g., all 15 f) reads as monotony. Shot-length VARIANCE is what predicts liking; alternate bursts with 45–75 f breath shots.
- Wall-to-wall maximum intensity. Without the 0:56 dip and the 1:28 / 1:57 silences, the climax and title cannot feel bigger.
- Reading under motion: cards over 7 words, paragraph cards, more than ~120 words in total, narrator text on screen over ~40% of runtime, or key-card cap height under ~7% of frame (unreadable on phones).
- 'And then' structure: a sequence of feature cards with no but/therefore causality is a slideshow, not a story.
- Benchmark-slide syndrome: static bar charts, numbers without a from→to comparison, two numbers per card, counters that land off the hit.
- Unverified or fabricated claims, or staged demos. Every proof card must trace to a published, verifiable source, and simulated footage must be labeled. Google's Dec-2023 Gemini demo backlash is the cautionary case. The '5 months' and all Opus 5.5 facts must come from the client's sources.
- Diluting the title drop by showing the full product name early. Brand the color and light from frame 0 and say the name at 1:58. Make a separate ad cut if the brand must appear in the first 5 s.
- BRAAM and riser inflation. More than 3 braams, or risers that don't resolve exactly on a hit or a cut to silence on the frame.
- Crossfades and slow dissolves in Acts 2–3. Use hard cuts, 2–6 f dips to black, and 2 f white flashes.
- Photosensitivity: strobing white flashes or flutter cuts at more than 3 large, high-contrast flashes per second break WCAG 2.3.1-style flash limits. Cap white flashes at ≤2/s and flutter between images of similar luminance.
- AV sync drift: picture landing even 2 f after the audio transient feels late and sloppy. Drive audio and video from one cue list and verify against a click track.
- AI-cliché imagery (glowing brains, robot hands, binary rain, generic neon). It cheapens the piece. Hold one coherent factory metaphor with consistent visual rules.
- No emotional anchor. Fully abstract scenes feel cold; carry a protagonist (the amber light) and a human presence through diegetic messages ('is this shipping friday?').
- Off-center subjects in sub-12-frame shots. The eye searches and energy turns into noise.
- Cold open that explains instead of intriguing, or runs past ~8–10 s.
- Too many open loops at once (more than 2 with no VO), or all loops closed before the title, which removes the reason to stay to the end.
- Inconsistent screen direction: progress must always move left→right, and threats toward the camera.
- More than 3 musical mood changes (incoherent) or fewer than 2 (monotone). Tempos that break the integer frame grid; prefer 120/150/100/75 BPM at 30 fps.
- Ending on a logo with no button, a button longer than ~8 s, or an end card lingering more than 3 s.

## EDITING: EDITING RHYTHM & TRANSITIONS for a 2:15, no-voiceover, code-generated launch trailer. Covers the beat grid, how cut frequency climbs act by act (a rising sawtooth, not a straight ramp), cutting on vs. off the beat, stopdowns and silence before the drop, text-card timing, and a frame-exact recipe for

### Principles
- Lock the music grid first and drive everything from one master timeline. At 30 fps and 120 BPM in 4/4: beat = 15 frames, bar = 60 frames = exactly 2.000 s, 8th note = 7.5 f (alternate 8/7), 16th = 3.75 f (pattern 4,4,4,3). Generate the picture edit list (EDL) and the audio event list from the same bar/beat table so cuts and hits can never drift. Trailer editors pick the music before cutting because it sets mood, pacing and the turning points (Paul Cartlich via Elwyn; Derek Lieu via PC Gamer).
- Energy is a rising sawtooth, not a ramp. Each act speeds up, then resets at a stopdown, and each reset starts faster than the last one. Target average shot length (ASL) by section: hook 0.50 s, Act 1 1.10 s, Act 2a 0.75 s, Act 2b 0.50 s, Act 3a 0.58 s, Act 3b 0.46 s, title 4 s, button 1.3 s. Overall about 0.65 s. Benchmarks: the Bourne Ultimatum trailer (2007) runs 0.78 s per shot; 1970s trailers ran 1.4 to 1.8 s (robservatory). Trailer ASL has fallen for 60 years, and shorter shots carry more in-frame motion (Temple study).
- Change rule (the fix for 'too slow'): something must change on screen at least every 2.0 s before 0:28 and at least every 1.0 s after it. A change is a cut, a new word, a camera stop, a light hit or an in-shot impact. No shot except the title and the end card holds longer than 60 f. Never open a shot on a camera at rest that then accelerates. Cut in motion: every shot starts at 50% or more of its peak camera speed.
- Hook inside 5 s: on frame 0 the image is already moving and a hit sounds, and readable words appear by 4.0 s. A short social-style opener of about 5 s stops the scroll (Jessica Fox via Slashfilm). Put a title card before the 5 s mark (Noam Kroll via Elwyn). Establish the world within 10 s (Rowney and Gonzales via Elwyn).
- Cuts are punctuation, ranked by weight. Bar downbeats carry structural change: a new idea, a drop, the title. Quarter-note beats drive montage. 8ths and 16ths appear only inside builds. Cuts on beats 2 and 4 or on the off-beat 'and' add groove and restlessness. Not every hit is a cut: about one third of hits should be in-shot events such as a camera slam-stop, an object impact or a light flare.
- The shorter the shot, the simpler the image. Any shot of 8 frames or less must be center-framed and either a silhouette or a recall of an image already shown. Fury Road cut 2,700 shots and kept the focal point on the frame's crosshair so the eye never has to search (Nedomansky via LaughingSquid). The brain can get gist from 13 ms of exposure (MIT), but meaning at montage speed needs a familiar image in a predictable position.
- Silence is the loudest instrument. Use 2 to 3 full stopdowns to silence, at act ends (composer forums: vi-control/TAXI). The biggest visual change of each act must land on the downbeat after the silence. A stopdown resets the ear and makes the next hit bigger (Rareform).
- Picture leads sound by 0 to 1 frame. Compute cut_frame = floor(t_audio x 30). Viewers tolerate audio lagging picture more than audio leading it: ITU-R BT.1359 puts detectability at 45 ms of audio lead against 125 ms of audio lag (Wikipedia, A/V sync).
- Every transition gets a sound, and every sound gets a picture event: a suck-back plus a hit on every card, and accents (a quick shot, an SFX or a music hit) between story beats. Lines cut back to back with no accents feel flat (Lieu and the Netflix mashup, both via Elwyn).
- Mostly show. With no voiceover, cards are the narrator, but keep them to 30% of screen time or less, 5 words or fewer, 28 characters or fewer. A card comes in on a hit and leaves on a hard cut. It never fades.
- Change camera angle and scale on every cut. Each consecutive pair of shots changes at least 2 of: pitch by 30 degrees or more, scale by 2x or more, screen direction. Top-down god's-eye shots show the system and its scale. Low worm's-eye shots show power and threat. Macro shows detail. That alternation is the 'top angle / bottom angle' feel the client asked for.
- Escalate spectacle and keep the best for last. Order features from simple to elaborate (Lieu, PC Gamer), and don't open at full power or you have nowhere to go (Skip Chaisson via Elwyn). The hook borrows Act 3 images only as sub-second flashes, so the full shots still pay off later.

### Techniques
- **Accelerando cut ladder (halving staircase)**. *When:* The 4 bars before the main drop (1:02 to 1:10) and before the title (1:50 to 1:58). Use a 2-bar version before minor stopdowns.
  *How:* At 120 BPM / 30 fps: bar 1 = 2 shots x 30 f; bar 2 = 4 x 15 f; bar 3 = 8 x (8,7) f; bar 4 = 16ths [4,4,4,3] for 1 beat (pre-drop) or 2 beats (pre-title), then 15 f of zoom-stutter (pre-drop only), then 30 f of SILENCE on black. Each tier halves the shot length; every bar stays exactly 60 f. Continuous form: d(t) = d0*(d1/d0)^(t/T), then snap each duration to the nearest grid value {60,30,15,8|7,4|3}. Audio follows the picture: a tom or kick on every cut, and a riser (noise plus a 2 to 4 octave pitch sweep over 4 bars) whose peak ends 1 frame before the silence. Tiers of 8 f or less use only recalls and centered silhouettes, with mean luminance matched within +/-8% so the run never reads as strobing.
  *Reference:* Bourne Ultimatum trailer (2007): about 165 cuts in 130 s (0.78 s per shot), the density ceiling to aim for. Battlefield 1 reveal (2016): cuts speed up into the slowed 'Seven Nation Army' riff drop. Steal the idea that the cut rate itself is the riser.
- **Stopdown / silence before the drop**. *When:* End of Act 1 (f810), end of Act 2a (f1365), mid Act 3 (f2730), and full-length before the drop (f2070) and the title (f3510).
  *How:* End the riser at its peak with no decay. Run a reverse-cymbal suck-back of 0.5 to 1.0 s that ends exactly where the silence starts. Silence length: 15 f (1 beat) mid-act, 30 f (2 beats) before the drop and the title, never more than 60 f. Do not go to digital zero for more than 0.5 s. Keep a floor at -45 to -55 dBFS (room tone or sub) or one intimate sound: a single clock tick, a keystroke, a breath. Picture during the silence: black, a near-frozen hero frame (1% motion or less), or one 1 to 2 word card. The return lands exactly on the downbeat: the hit sits 15 to 25 dB above the silence floor, the trailer's biggest scale and luminance change happens on the same frame, and an overexposure flash fires.
  *Reference:* Joker teaser (2019): silence on the Arkham elevator shot (Elwyn). Avengers: Endgame teaser (2018): silence and black before the title. Oppenheimer trailer (2023): builds to silence. Steal the restraint: the bigger the next moment, the cleaner the silence.
- **The drop reveal**. *When:* Act 3 starts at f2100 (1:10.0): the solution, the software factory and Claude Opus 5.5.
  *How:* One 60 f hold, the only 2 s shot after 0:28, with the camera moving: a push or crane at 8 to 10% scale per second. Low angle: camera 0.2 to 0.5 m off the ground, pitched up 15 to 30 degrees, Three.js fov 55 to 70. Then a 30 f top-angle counter shot (pitch -90 degrees, fov 20 to 30), then a 30 f macro, then a 45 f name-card slam on bar 38. Audio on f2100: braam or sub-drop plus impact plus full drums together. Visual on f2100: a 1 f white overexposure plus a 4 f decay.
  *Reference:* Inception trailer 2 (2010): the braam lands on the reveal at about 0:24 (Rareform). Godzilla (2014) teaser: scale shown by a low-angle reveal. Steal: one sound, one image, held long enough to feel the size.
- **Smash cut**. *When:* Problem to solution, quiet to loud, loud to silence. Use 3 to 5 per trailer: hook card to Act 1, each act start after a stopdown, title to button.
  *How:* Zero transition frames. Change at least 2 of these on the same frame: loudness by 12 dB or more (or into silence), mean luminance by 40% or more, scale by 4x (macro to wide), color temperature (cool blue 'problem world' to warm amber 'factory'). Never soften it with a dissolve.
  *Reference:* Hobbs & Shaw trailer 2: hard 'face hits' on jokes (Elwyn). Steal: the cut and the hit are a single event.
- **Match cut (graphic / motion / concept), solved in screen space**. *When:* To carry the story across a jump in scale or time: one agent to a fleet; a terminal to a factory floor; a red failing test to a green station light.
  *How:* In the last frame of shot A, project subject X to screen space (centroid, bounding size, velocity). In shot B, solve the camera so subject Y's centroid sits within 5% of frame width of X, its size within 10%, and its velocity within 20 degrees of the same direction. Cut on the frame of peak velocity so motion hides any mismatch. Optionally overlay the shared shape across the cut for 2 f. Ideas: a blinking caret becomes a laser line on the line; a diff-view grid becomes the top-down factory grid; one node becomes 1,000 nodes in a zoom-out match.
  *Reference:* 2001: A Space Odyssey, bone to satellite. Lawrence of Arabia, match flame to desert sunrise. Steal the 'idea becomes industry' compression in a single frame.
- **Whip pan / whip tilt**. *When:* Location or angle changes at speed in Acts 2b and 3. A whip tilt moves from a top angle to a bottom angle in one gesture.
  *How:* 8 to 12 f in total. The outgoing camera accelerates its yaw (or pitch) with easeInCubic over 4 to 6 f up to 25 to 45 degrees per frame. Cut at the peak. The incoming shot starts at the same angular velocity in the same direction and decelerates with easeOutCubic over 4 to 6 f. Motion blur: accumulate 16 to 32 subframes over a 180-degree shutter. Whoosh SFX of 0.4 to 0.7 s with its peak on the cut frame, so about 65% of the whoosh plays before the cut.
  *Reference:* Edgar Wright's whip transitions (Hot Fuzz, Scott Pilgrim). Steal direction continuity: every whip in a sequence goes the same way until the story turns.
- **Speed ramp (time remap)**. *When:* Impacts and hero moments in Acts 2b and 3. At most 1 ramp per about 8 s, 6 or fewer in total.
  *How:* Speed s(f): 1.0 drops to 0.15 to 0.25 within 3 to 4 f (easeOutExpo) on the impact frame, holds 12 to 24 f, then climbs to 2.5 to 4.0 over 6 to 10 f (easeInCubic) into a cut or whip. Scene time: tau[f] = tau[f-1] + s(f)/30. Drive everything from tau: the camera spline parameter, particles and shader uniforms. Sample motion-blur subframes across tau, so blur shrinks in slow motion and streaks in fast. Audio: on the slowdown, a low-pass sweep from 18 kHz to 2 kHz plus a 1-octave tape-stop over 0.25 s; on the speed-up, a reverse whoosh into the next hit.
  *Reference:* 300 (2006) trailer; Matrix Resurrections trailer (2021). Steal: slow on the impact, fast into the cut, never slow out of the cut.
- **White flash / overexposure flash (and delayed-boom variant)**. *When:* Only on the drop, the name card, the title and the final button hit, 6 or fewer in total.
  *How:* Frame 0: additive white at 100% (or exposure +3 stops, or bloom x6), then decay of 0.55, 0.30, 0.15, 0.07 over the next 4 f. Pair it with the impact. Delayed-boom variant: the flash on frame f, silence for 15 to 30 f, then the boom (light reaches you before sound). Safety: never more than 3 luminance flashes in any 30-frame window (WCAG 2.3.1).
  *Reference:* Oppenheimer trailer: light before sound. Steal the delayed-boom for the Opus 5.5 reveal.
- **Black-frame breaths and cut-to-black ring-outs**. *When:* Act boundaries, before and after cards, after the final button hit.
  *How:* 6 to 12 f of black between sections, carrying the reverb tail of the last hit. 15 to 30 f of black during silences. A 45 f black ring-out after the button's final hit. Use a 2 to 4 f 'blink' dip-to-black between unrelated shots in Act 1 only. Always hard cut to black and back. The only fade allowed is the last 15 f of the end card.
  *Reference:* Joker teaser: each title and cut to black gets a cymbal riser, slam or whoosh (Elwyn). Steal: black is a beat, not an absence.
- **Text card timing and word-per-beat stacking**. *When:* All copy: problem statements, the story beats, the model name, the title.
  *How:* Hold time once the text is fully on: hold_f = max(15, round(9 + 1.5 x characters)). That is 20 characters per second (Netflix's adult reading limit) plus about 0.3 s to recognize the shape. Results: 1 word of 6 characters or fewer = 15 to 18 f; 3 words of about 13 characters = 30 f (2 beats); a 27-character line = 45 to 60 f. A card already seen once holds at 0.6x. Animate in over 4 to 8 f with easeOutExpo (cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)), scale 1.12 to 1.0, blur 12 px to 0. Slam variant: scale 1.6 to 1.0 in 4 f (easeOutQuart) plus a 2 to 4 px shake. Exit on a hard cut at the next accent. Word stacking: one word per beat (15 f each, accumulating), then hold 1 beat, so 3 words fill one 60 f bar and something changes every 0.5 s. Split longer lines into 2 cards on consecutive downbeats.
  *Reference:* Netflix 'Save the Date' mashup: a suck-back plus a hit on every title card (Elwyn). Skip Chaisson's Daredevil sizzle: '1973. New York. Hell's Kitchen.' / 'Fear.' Steal fragment cards on beats.
- **Card sandwich and data cards (storytelling without voiceover)**. *When:* Act 2b: the story of the first wave, where coding agents took about 5 months to go from capability to budget.
  *How:* Alternate image, card, image, card, where each card carries a fragment of one sentence. Numbers are rolling counters: digits roll for 6 to 10 f, then lock with a click plus a hit. Chapter montage with shrinking chapters: MONTH 1 to 5 chapters of 6, 5, 5, 4 and 4 beats (90/75/75/60/60 f). Each chapter opens with a 15 f MONTH card on a tick, and density climbs from 4 to 6 events per chapter. The shrinking chapters are themselves the acceleration.
  *Reference:* The Social Network trailer (2010, Mark Woollen): one logline split across images, over a choir cover of 'Creep'. The first cut was built from stock photos and family pictures. Dunkirk trailer (2017): a tempo-locked ticking watch and a Shepard tone. Steal: text plus a ticking pulse can tell a whole story.
- **Stutter edit (frame-repeat / step-print / zoom stutter)**. *When:* The last beat of a build before the drop and the title, 4 times or fewer in total.
  *How:* Frame-repeat: play frames n, n+1, n, n+1 as a 2-frame loop for 8 to 15 f. Step-print: hold each rendered frame for 2 to 3 frames (10 to 15 Hz staccato) for 0.5 to 1 s. Zoom stutter: scale jumps +3% every 2 f with no tween. Audio: gate the riser at 1/16 (3.75 f), then 1/32 (about 1.9 f), and repeat the last 60 to 120 ms slice 4 to 8 times. Never alternate image and black: that is a strobe.
  *Reference:* Battlefield 1 reveal (2016): stutters into the riff. Steal: the stutter is the inhale before the hit.
- **Glitch transition**. *When:* Moments tied to AI or compute, and the old way breaking. About 5 or fewer in total, never on the main drop.
  *How:* 3 to 6 f. Frame 1: RGB split of 6 to 12 px horizontally. Frame 2: 8 to 20 horizontal slices displaced by +/-2 to 8% of frame width. Frame 3: a 32 to 64 px macroblock mosaic mixing shots A and B (a datamosh look). Frame 4: shot B clean, with a 2 px leftover split that decays. Audio: a 50 to 120 ms bitcrushed burst (8-bit, sample-and-hold at 4 to 6 kHz) plus a 1/32 stutter.
  *Reference:* Cyberpunk 2077 trailers and the Ex Machina trailer (2014): glitch that belongs to the subject. Steal: glitch means 'machine', so ration it.
- **Impact frame + camera shake + zoom punch**. *When:* The top 4 to 5 hits: drop, name card, title, final button.
  *How:* 1 to 2 f of a posterized or inverted silhouette (anime-style impact frame), then the shot. Shake: amplitude A(t) = A0 x e^(-t/tau), with A0 = 1.5 to 3% of frame height on big hits (0.5 to 1% on small ones), tau = 4 to 6 f, noise at 12 to 20 Hz, rotation +/-1 degree. Zoom punch: an instant +4 to 8% at the hit, easing back over 8 to 12 f (easeOutCubic). Chromatic aberration: 6 px to 0 over 6 f. Inverted frames count as flashes in the WCAG count.
  *Reference:* Anime sakuga impact frames; Hobbs & Shaw added hits. Steal: the frame itself flinches.
- **Hip-hop micro-montage (one sound per image)**. *When:* Showing the factory line run end to end (spec, code, test, review, merge, deploy) in about 1.5 s. Use 2 to 3 times in Act 3.
  *How:* 4 to 8 shots of 4 to 8 f each, all center-framed. Each shot gets its own distinct SFX (click, clunk, servo, stamp, chime) placed on 8ths or 16ths. Close on a 15 to 30 f anchor shot.
  *Reference:* Requiem for a Dream (Aronofsky named the technique and compared it to hip-hop sampling); Edgar Wright's Hot Fuzz and Shaun of the Dead montages. Steal: a process shown as percussion.
- **Recall flurry**. *When:* The 16th-note tier of the final accelerando (bar 59, f3480 to f3509).
  *How:* 8 shots already seen in the hook and Acts 1 to 3, at 3 to 4 f each, in story order: the whole trailer replayed in 1 s. Match luminance within +/-8% to stay under the flash limit.
  *Reference:* Common in game-trailer finales. Steal: recall is the only content that reads at 4 f.
- **Pre-lap (J-cut) and telegraphed sound**. *When:* Act 1 and into every reveal: momentum without cutting faster.
  *How:* Start the next scene's sound 8 to 30 f before its picture: factory hum before the reveal, ticking before the MONTH montage. Telegraphing: play an SFX with no picture, then repeat it one bar later with the picture.
  *Reference:* Tommy Malatesta's Hereditary trailer (via Elwyn). Steal: sound arrives first and the image confirms it.
- **Occlusion wipe / pass-by**. *When:* Moving between factory stations in Act 3.
  *How:* A foreground object (robot arm, panel, wall of particles) crosses the lens and fills 90% of the frame or more for 2 to 4 f. Hide the cut inside the occlusion. Center a Doppler whoosh (pitch falling 3 to 5 semitones) on the cut.
  *Reference:* Top Gun: Maverick (jet pass-bys). Steal: the cut hides behind something huge.
- **Flash-forward hook**. *When:* 0:00 to 0:06.
  *How:* f0 to f119: 10 shots borrowed from Act 3, durations [15,15,15,15,15,15,8,7,8,7], all centered, with a hit on f0. f120: smash to black plus the thesis card held 45 f. f165 to f179: 15 f of black breath with the reverb tail. Alternative: a question card plus one look, as in Joker's 9 s cold open (Elwyn).
  *Reference:* The roughly 5 s 'social opener' (Slashfilm / Mark Woollen & Associates). Steal: show the payoff before the setup, but too fast to spend it.
- **Syncopated push cut**. *When:* Act 2a ('still the problem'), and the bar before each drop.
  *How:* Cut on the 'and' of 4 (7 to 8 f before the downbeat) and hold across the barline. This leans forward and reads as restless. Alternate backbeat cuts (beats 2 and 4) with downbeat cuts. In the bar before a drop, put cuts on off-beats only, so the downbeat drop feels like a release.
  *Reference:* Music-video practice: move cuts off 1 and 3 onto 2, 4 and the 'and' (No Film School). Steal: tension equals cuts that arrive early.
- **Button / false ending ('one more thing')**. *When:* 2:02 to 2:10, right after the title.
  *How:* 15 f of black, then a 75 f quiet shot (the only slow camera in the second half, 1 to 2% push per second) under a single tick or ping. Then a 45 f card, a 45 f punch shot, a 15 f final-hit image, and a 45 f black ring-out. The end card comes last.
  *Reference:* Mad Max: Fury Road trailer ('What a lovely day!'); Joker's closing smirk. Steal: one last surprise after the audience thinks it's over, here the next wave.
- **Angle-alternation montage (top / bottom / macro)**. *When:* Every montage cluster.
  *How:* Cluster = 3 to 5 shots plus 1 anchor held 1 to 2 beats longer. Example rotation: Top (pitch -90, fov 20 to 30) to Macro (fov 15 to 25, shallow depth of field) to Low hero (camera 0.2 to 0.5 m high, pitch +15 to 30, fov 55 to 70) to Side tracking (fov 35 to 45). Never place two consecutive shots at the same height and scale. Keep screen direction consistent within a cluster (left to right reads as progress) and flip it only at a story turn. Module of 2 bars = a dense bar [15,15,15,8,7] plus a breathing bar [30,15,15].
  *Reference:* Fury Road (crosshair framing for readability at speed); Dune (2020/2024) trailers (god's-eye vs. worm's-eye scale contrast). Steal: the angle changes more than the content does.

=== 5. NUMERIC RULES ===
- Minimum shot: 3 f for recalls, 7 f for new but simple centered images, 12 f for a new image that must be understood.
- Maximum shot: 60 f anywhere except title and end card. After 0:28, 45 f max except the 60 f drop reveal.
- Dead time (no cut, word or in-shot event): 2.0 s max before 0:28, 1.0 s max after.
- Every module of 8 bars (16 s) or more must contain a stopdown, a hold, or a drop. Modern trailers run 5-7 distinct sections (Rareform).
- Silence: 15 f mid-act, 30 f before the drop and the title, never over 60 f. Floor -45 to -55 dBFS, or one intimate sound.
- Hits: at least 1 audio accent per cut in Acts 2b-3; about 30-40% of hits are in-shot (no cut).
- Cards: 5 words / 28 characters max, 30% of screen time or less. hold_f = max(15, 9 + 1.5*chars), counted after the animate-in (4-8 f). Recalled cards x0.6. In on a hit, out on a hard cut.
- Flashes: 6 white flashes or fewer in total, and 3 or fewer luminance flashes in any 30-f window (WCAG 2.3.1: a flash is a pair of opposing changes of at least 10% relative luminance with the darker state below 0.8, over 25% or more of the 10-degree field; saturated red counts separately).
- Camera: dolly scale rate 2-4%/s (Act 1), 6-12%/s (Act 3). Shots start at 50% or more of peak velocity. At 30 fps avoid medium-speed pans (judder): go slow (cinematographers' rule of thumb is 7 s or more to cross frame) or whip with heavy blur.
- Motion blur: 180-degree shutter, 8-16 subframes normally, 32 for whips. Sample over remapped time tau.

=== 6. EASING CHEAT SHEET ===
Text in: easeOutExpo cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1), 4-8 f. Slam: easeOutQuart, scale 1.6 to 1.0 in 4 f. Text out: hard cut (or easeInExpo cubic-bezier(0.7,0,0.84,0) in 3-4 f).
Camera on holds: constant velocity or easeOutSine, never easeIn at a shot head. Do not ease-in-out every camera move: it makes every shot start and end still, which reads as slow.
Speed ramp: easeOutExpo to slow (3-4 f), easeInCubic to fast (6-10 f).
Whip: easeInCubic out of A, easeOutCubic into B, matched angular velocity at the cut.
Zoom punch: +4-8% instant, easeOutCubic back over 8-12 f. Shake: A0*exp(-t/tau), tau 4-6 f, 12-20 Hz noise.
Flash decay per frame: 1.0, 0.55, 0.30, 0.15, 0.07.

=== 7. CODE RECIPES (pseudocode) ===
accel(beatsOf16th=1): tiers=[[30,30],[15]*4,[8,7]*4,[4,4,4,3]*beatsOf16th]; then pad to the bar with [15 STUT,30 SIL] (pre-drop) or [30 SIL] (pre-title).
remap: tau[0]=0; tau[f]=tau[f-1]+speed(f)/30. Render scene(tau[f]); blur subframes at tau[f]+k*speed(f)/(30*2*N).
shake(f,f0): A=A0*exp(-(f-f0)/tau); dx=A*noise(f*freq/30,0); dy=A*noise(0,f*freq/30); rot=A*0.3*noise(...).
cardHold(text)=max(15, round(9+1.5*len(text))).
flashCheck: per frame, L = mean relative luminance of the worst 25% region. Count opposing transitions with dL >= 0.1 and min(L) < 0.8 in a sliding 30-f window; assert count <= 3 (a flash = 2 transitions).
deadTimeCheck: per-frame mean abs diff plus the event list. Flag any window over the limit with no cut, word or impact.
Calibration: run PySceneDetect (ContentDetector) on the reference trailers below and compare their per-act ASL with section 2.

### Pitfalls
- One cut rate for more than 8 bars becomes a 'wall of noise' (Rowney and Gonzales) and numbs the viewer as much as slowness does. Alternate dense bars with breathing bars, and reset at stopdowns.
- Ease-in-out on every camera and text move. Each shot then starts and ends nearly still, so even a fast-cut piece feels slow. This is the likely cause of v1's 'extremely slow' feel. Cut in motion instead.
- Dissolves, crossfades and slow fades to black in the middle of the trailer instantly read as a corporate explainer. Use hard cuts, smash cuts, whips, match cuts, flashes and occasional glitches. Fade only the last 15 f.
- Text overload: cards over 5 words, cards held 3 s or longer, cards that don't land on hits, or cards above about 30% of screen time. The client asked for something visual-centric, so cards are punctuation, not paragraphs.
- Putting new information into shots of 8 f or less, or framing them off-center. Nobody can read it. Ultra-short shots must be centered silhouettes or recalls.
- Photosensitive-seizure risk: alternating bright and dark images in 16th-note cuts, black/white stutters, inverted impact frames back to back, or saturated red flashes. Keep 3 or fewer flashes in any 1 s (WCAG 2.3.1) and match montage luminance within +/-8%.
- Silence that is too long (over 2 s) or true digital zero reads as a playback bug on the web. Silence with no payoff on the next downbeat wastes the tension.
- A drop landing on a weak image, mid-shot or a frame late. The drop frame must be a cut plus the trailer's biggest scale and luminance change, with picture leading audio by 0-1 frame, never trailing it.
- Opening at full power with nowhere to go (Skip Chaisson). The hook previews Act 3 only as sub-second flashes, and each act must outdo the last.
- Leaning on clichés: a braam on every hit, glitch on every transition, a ticking clock everywhere. Ration them: braams at the drop and the title only, about 5 glitches or fewer, ticking only in the MONTH chapters.
- Consecutive shots at the same angle and scale create accidental jump cuts and visual monotony. Change at least 2 of pitch, scale and direction on every cut.
- Medium-speed pans at 30 fps judder. Go slow, or whip fast with heavy subframe blur.
- Title too early (under about 80% of runtime) or a post-title tail over about 17 s. Everything after the title then feels like end credits.
- Non-deterministic timing: animation driven by wall-clock time, or 8th notes rounded inconsistently (7.5 f). Derive everything from frame index to tau, alternate 8/7 and 4,4,4,3 so every bar is exactly 60 f, and generate audio and picture from one EDL.
- Cutting only on downbeats is predictable and square. Mix in backbeat (2 and 4) and off-beat push cuts in Act 2. Keep downbeats for structural moments.

### Principles
- THE FIRST 3 SECONDS CARRY ABOUT HALF THE VALUE. Nielsen research for Meta (mobile feed, as secondhand reports describe it) found about 47% of a video ad's value is delivered in the first 3 s and about 74% by 10 s. Facebook also reported that 65% of people who watch 3 s go on to watch 10 s. Wistia cut a long intro to under 10 s and halved the early viewer loss. Rule: frame 0 is the hook. No logo, no fade-in from black, no camera easing out of rest, no 'title sequence'.
- ORDER IS GENRE, THEN HOOK, THEN CONTENT (Derek Lieu). Within about 5 s the viewer must know what world this is (AI agents building software). The hook ('the next wave is a factory') must land within 10–15 s, and the poster frame's promise must be confirmed immediately. The MrBeast handbook's version: the first minute exists to prove the thumbnail's promise will be met.
- ENERGY IS CONTRAST, NOT CONSTANT LOUDNESS. Lieu's intensity graph climbs with breathers. If everything is loud, nothing is. Every big peak (braam, reveal) needs a valley just before it (0.4–1.0 s of silence or near-black). 'Bam-bam-bam' only reads as bam if the gaps between hits vary.
- CURIOSITY IS A GAP THE VIEWER CAN ALMOST CLOSE (Loewenstein's information-gap theory). Start from what executives already lived through (the coding-agent wave went from capability to budget in about 5 months) and open a gap they can't close themselves: what is the next wave? Chain the loops so each answer opens a bigger question. The chain is the story's spine: every beat links to the next by BUT or THEREFORE, never AND THEN.
- CHANGE IS THE CURRENCY OF ATTENTION, BUT ONLY RELATED CHANGE BUILDS COMPREHENSION. In Lang's LC4MP, cuts and other structural features grab attention automatically. Related cuts help memory. Rapid unrelated cuts, and fast pacing combined with arousing content, can overload the viewer and reduce encoding. Cut fast when shots share a motif, protagonist or palette. Slow the picture down when text carries the meaning.
- READ OR WATCH, NEVER BOTH AT FULL LOAD. While a must-read card is on screen, the 3D background quiets down: slow camera, darker exposure, no new focal objects. Between cards, spectacle can run wild. This one rule lets you be fast and intelligible together.
- REPEATED EFFECTS STOP WORKING. Potter et al. (2018) found that repeated jingles and production effects lost their orienting response, while voice changes kept theirs. With no voiceover we have no voice change to lean on, so every repeated hit, whoosh, flash or shake must be varied, and the total count of each must be budgeted.
- SHOW, DON'T TELL, AND ADD THE 'RIGHT-BRAIN' FEATURES. Orlando Wood (System1) links ads with words on screen, abstract settings and no characters to weaker effectiveness. Ads with a character, a story, a sense of place, melody and implicit meaning do better. A kinetic-typography piece over abstract 3D is the textbook flat ad, so counter it on purpose: a protagonist (one agent 'spark'), a physical place (the factory), a melodic motif, and a final line the viewer completes in their own head.
- ESCALATE ON SEVERAL AXES AT ONCE. Scale (count of agents ×10 per act), speed (shot length, tempo), loudness (+3–4 LU per act), brightness and saturation, and how extreme the camera angle is. Each act must beat the previous one on at least 2 axes, or it reads as a plateau.
- FOR EXECUTIVES, INTELLIGENCE MEANS SPECIFICITY PLUS RESTRAINT. Use concrete nouns from their world (review queue, CI, deploy, on-call), numbers with units, and wit instead of adjectives, and let them draw the last inference. Motivated viewers put more effort into relevant messages (LC4MP), so you can push density when every card is specific. Generic hype loses them at any speed.
- PEAK AND END DOMINATE MEMORY (Kahneman's peak-end rule). Put the biggest audiovisual moment at 80–88% of runtime. Then a false stop, an end title of 5 s or less, and a button. Never recap: Wistia found that recaps and sign-offs cause drop-off before the end.
- LENGTH IS PART OF THE BUDGET. In Wistia's data (564k videos), engagement stays roughly flat up to about 2:00 and then falls off noticeably. Aim for 2:10–2:15, and let only the climax and end live past 2:00.
- CUT ON COMPREHENSION, NOT ON COMPLETION. Leave a shot about 4–8 frames after its meaning lands, not when its animation finishes. Vary rhythm with a 1/f-like spread of shot lengths (Cutting et al. 2010), not a metronome.
- EDIT FOR THE EYE. After a cut, gaze defaults toward the centre and follows the direction of the camera's perspective shift (Smith; Goller et al.). In fast runs, keep the focal point in the centre third or at the same screen position across the cut, keep screen direction consistent, and put text wherever the eye already is.

### Techniques
- **Frame-zero cold open (in medias res)**. *When:* 0:00–0:03, and the first frame of every act
  *How:* Frame 0 is a full-intensity image already in motion. The camera curve starts at 60% or more of cruising speed: sample a linear or easeOutQuart curve from t=0.25, never from rest. An audio transient also sits on frame 0: sub drop sine 80→35 Hz over 250 ms with exponential decay, plus a 10 ms noise click high-passed at 2 kHz, peaking at -3 dBFS. Add a 2-frame bloom flash (frame 0 at 100% additive white or bloom ×4, frame 1 at 40%). First text slams in at frames 9–15, 3 words or fewer, and carries the stake or number. Muted-autoplay check: the image alone, with no sound, must raise a question. No logo or wordmark before about 55% of runtime.
  *Reference:* Lieu's cold opens start on an intense moment. Joker's teaser uses a 9-second cold open built on a question and a look (per Jonny Elwyn's breakdown). Noam Kroll opened his social trailer with 'aggressive flashback shots' instead of easing in. Steal: no warm-up, start at speed.
- **Stakes-first number card**. *When:* First card at about 0.4 s, then one hero number per act at most
  *How:* One number plus one unit, 3 words or fewer ('5 MONTHS.'). Cap height 72–140 px at 1080p so it survives phone playback at about 0.2× scale. Hold 24–33 f. Decode it immediately in two stacked cards ('FROM "IT CAN CODE"' / 'TO "IT\'S IN THE BUDGET."'). The number is something the exec half-knows, which opens a gap they can almost close.
  *Reference:* Apple silicon reveal films treat a giant number as the hero of a nearly empty card. Steal the scale ratio: number at about 3× the size of its label.
- **Front-loaded wow tease (flash-forward)**. *When:* Tease at 0:06–0:08; full payoff at about 55% of runtime (the factory reveal)
  *How:* Show 45–60 f of the most spectacular shot (the full factory from a god's-eye angle) but degraded: fog density ×2, exposure -1 stop, frame only partly revealed. Hard-cut away on a hit before it resolves. Pay it off later with the identical camera path, fog cleared, lights on. The viewer is now waiting for a picture they have half-seen.
  *Reference:* The MrBeast handbook's 'wow factor' (the house craned in about 30 s into the video) and its practice of frontloading the best moments. Trailer cold opens are routinely pulled from the third act.
- **Open-loop ladder on a BUT/THEREFORE spine**. *When:* Script stage, before any animation
  *How:* Write the beat list as one chain where each link is BUT or THEREFORE, with zero AND THEN links (audit this mechanically). Loops: A, '5 MONTHS.' (opens 0:00, closes 0:05). B, 'THAT WAS THE FIRST WAVE.' (opens 0:09, closes about 1:17 at the factory reveal). C, 'SO WHAT CAN?' (opens about 1:12, closes about 1:40 at the product reveal). D, the button 'MONTH 1.', left open on purpose. No loop stays open longer than about 70 s. Add a fresh micro-question or payoff every 15–20 s; this is the MrBeast 're-engagement' interval (minutes 3 and 6 of a long video) scaled down to 2:15.
  *Reference:* Trey Parker and Matt Stone's 'therefore/but' rule. Lieu: pose questions, answer some, leave some open (via Jonny Elwyn).
- **Ticking clock plus Shepard tone (tension that cannot resolve)**. *When:* The whole problem act (about 0:38–1:12), dropping out completely at the hard stop
  *How:* Tick: 2 ms noise impulse through a resonant bandpass at 3.2 kHz (Q about 8), -18 dBFS, on 8th notes at 120 BPM (4 per second). Switch to 16ths for the last 8 s. Shepard tone: 9 sine partials an octave apart, amplitude Gaussian over log2(f) centred on 440 Hz with sigma 1.5 octaves, all gliding up at 1 octave per 10 s with wrap-around, level ramping -24 → -14 dBFS across the act. Visual partner: a queue counter on screen that ticks up on beats (10 → 50 → 200 → 1,000).
  *Reference:* Dunkirk (2017): Nolan built the score around his own pocket-watch ticking and a Shepard tone. Steal the relentless, non-resolving rise during the problem dwell.
- **Accents between cards**. *When:* Any time two text cards would otherwise sit back to back
  *How:* Insert a 4–12 f accent between cards: a macro detail, a UI glyph, a light streak, or a pure SFX or music hit. Pattern: CARD (30–45 f) → ACCENT (6–10 f) → CARD. Never stack more than 3 cards without at least 8 f of image between them. Accents must belong to the same motif or scene (related cuts) so they add energy without adding cognitive load.
  *Reference:* Derek Lieu's 'accents': quick shots, sound effects, short interjections, music hits and transitions. He notes that editors who stack lines without accents drain the energy (via Jonny Elwyn).
- **Beat-stacked card splitting ('bam-bam-bam' copy)**. *When:* Any line over 4 words; every energy beat
  *How:* Split sentences into cards of 1–3 words, each landing on a beat. At 120 BPM a beat is 15 f: a 1–2 word card gets 15 f, a 3-word card 30 f. The last card of a stack holds at least 24 f. Floor of 8 f per single word (about 225 wpm in rapid serial presentation), and only for short, predictable words of 8 characters or fewer. Entrance in 3–5 f: easeOutExpo, i.e. cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1), scale 1.12→1.00, opacity 0→1 over 2 f. Downbeats only: a 1 f bloom pop plus 4–8 px screen shake decaying with a 0.12 s time constant. Exit by hard cut (0 f) or a 3 f scale-up with blur. No typewriter reveals except inside depicted UI or code.
  *Reference:* 'CODE GOT FAST.' | 'SHIPPING' | 'DIDN'T.' Jonny Elwyn's trailer breakdown notes each title arriving with a cymbal riser plus a slam or whoosh.
- **One-channel load balancing (read OR watch)**. *When:* Every must-read card that sits over 3D
  *How:* While the card is up: camera angular velocity at most 5°/s, or background translation at most 5% of frame width per second. Background exposure -0.7 to -1.0 stop under the text, with a radial vignette. No new focal objects enter. Music drops a layer or holds its chord. Text contrast at least 7:1. When no text is on screen, unleash it: background movement of 20–60% of frame width per second, fast cuts. Within fast montages, keep shots semantically related (same protagonist, colour or motif).
  *Reference:* LC4MP: related cuts aid memory; rapid unrelated cuts, and fast pacing combined with arousing content, can push viewers into overload.
- **Variable cadence with designated holds (1/f rhythm)**. *When:* Assembling the edit decision list
  *How:* Target average shot length (ASL): cold open 1.0–1.5 s, Act I 1.6–2.2 s, Act II 1.0–1.5 s, Act III 0.5–0.9 s, final peak bar 6–10 f per shot. Draw shot lengths from a pink-noise sequence quantised to beats (1–4 beats; half-beats in Act III). Coefficient of variation per act 0.4–0.8. No more than 4 consecutive shots of equal length unless it is a deliberate stutter. At most 4 designated holds in the film, each 2.5–4.0 s and each kept alive with a 2–4% push-in or drift. Expect roughly 100–120 shots in 2:15, an overall ASL of about 1.1–1.3 s.
  *Reference:* Cutting, DeLong and Nothelfer (2010): shot-length sequences in modern films approach a 1/f pattern, so variability matters as much as speed. The Mad Max: Fury Road trailers tighten cut rate across the piece.
- **Silence drop and false stop**. *When:* Before the factory reveal and before the product reveal, plus one false stop just before the end title. Three in total.
  *How:* 6–12 f of 'suck' (a reversed reverb swell cut off sharply), then 12–30 f of true silence (or room tone at -60 dBFS) over black or near-black, then the impact on the downbeat frame: braam plus sub at 30–45 Hz plus a flash. Never more than 1.5 s of continuous silence, because on social autoplay it reads as a broken video. A card shown inside the silence (e.g. 'SO WHAT CAN?') is read with total attention.
  *Reference:* Oppenheimer trailers hold quiet before the blast. The Joker teaser's soundless scream in the elevator shot shows the restraint (via Jonny Elwyn).
- **Lights-on reveal plus powers-of-ten escalation**. *When:* Opening of Act III (the factory) and the climax
  *How:* Lights-on: 6–8 banks of area lights switch on in sequence, one per beat. Each goes 0→1 intensity in 2 f with a 1.3× overshoot and a bloom spike, paired with a metallic clunk varied by ±2 semitones; the last bank lands on the downbeat with a braam. Powers of ten: camera height h(t) = h0·10^(t/T) with T = 1.2–1.6 s (×10 every 1.2–1.6 s). Three decades (agent → station → floor → campus) in about 4.5 s. Join scale changes with match-on-position cuts and show an agent counter (1 → 10 → 1,000).
  *Reference:* Eames, 'Powers of Ten' (1977), for the exponential zoom. Inception trailers' folding-city shot for a single 'wow' scale image.
- **Angle grammar: top for control, bottom for awe and threat**. *When:* Everywhere. This is the client's explicit 'top angle / bottom angle' request, given a meaning.
  *How:* Worm's-eye (camera 0.2–1.0 m high, FOV 70–90°, pitched up 25–45°) means threat or awe: backlog towers, the factory's height. God's-eye (straight down, FOV 25–35°, near-orthographic) means system and control: flows, fleets, the line. Macro (FOV 15–25°, shallow depth of field) means intimacy: the spark, code glyphs. Profile tracking means flow. Never use the same angle class 3 times in a row. In the climax, alternate top and bottom on every beat (12 f at 150 BPM).
  *Reference:* Tron: Legacy trailer for top-down light grids. Blade Runner 2049 trailers for god's-eye city vistas. Interstellar for low-angle scale against tiny humans.
- **Mirror callback: jammed stations become flowing stations**. *When:* The Act III station tour
  *How:* Re-render each Act II jammed station with the identical camera path and composition, now flowing. Same word, changed treatment: 'REVIEW.' in red, static and queued becomes 'REVIEW.' in white, moving and clear. 4–7 stations at 1 bar each (48 f at 150 BPM). Executives read the before and after with no explanation. This is the most 'intelligent' beat in the film.
  *Reference:* Classic setup and payoff; Apple's before-and-after comparison sequences.
- **Centre-framed fast cutting plus match-on-position**. *When:* Any run of shots shorter than 20 f
  *How:* Keep the focal point inside the centre 30% of frame, or at the previous shot's focal coordinates within ±5% of frame width. Keep screen direction consistent (left to right means forward progress). Text appears where the eye already is. Prefer cutting when the eye is at rest on centre.
  *Reference:* Mad Max: Fury Road: George Miller and DP John Seale centre-framed so viewers could follow very fast cutting (Vashi Nedomansky's 'Center Framed' essay). Goller et al.: first fixations after a cut follow the direction of the perspective change.
- **Audio pre-lap (J-cut) and ring-out (L-cut)**. *When:* Act transitions and every major change of scene or scale
  *How:* Start the next scene's sound 4–10 f before its picture; this pulls the viewer forward over the cut. Let impacts ring out 0.5–2 s across the next 1–3 shots so cuts feel joined into one gesture. Risers end on the exact hit frame, or 6–12 f early into a suck-gap for extra punch.
  *Reference:* Standard trailer mixing. Thomas Grove Carter: 'Building up a rich sound bed was key to selling the mood' (via Jonny Elwyn).
- **Anti-habituation variation and an effects budget**. *When:* All SFX and visual effects
  *How:* Never the identical SFX twice in a row: randomise pitch ±1–3 semitones, filter cutoff ±20%, and which layer is used; rotate 3–4 hit families (sub kick at 45–60 Hz, noise snap at 1–5 kHz, inharmonic metal at 300–3000 Hz, reversed swell). Music changes something every 4 bars (a new layer, a filter opening, a register change, a drop). Budget for 2:15: braams 3; silence drops 3; flash frames 10 or fewer and never more than 3 in any 1 s window (WCAG 2.3.1 / photosensitive-epilepsy safety); glitches 6 or fewer, in the problem act only; shakes 8 or fewer; whip transitions 6 or fewer.
  *Reference:* Potter, Sites, Jamison-Koenig and Zheng (2018): repeated jingles and production effects lost their orienting response through habituation; voice changes did not.
- **Protagonist spark (right-brain anchor)**. *When:* The whole film: at least 6 appearances
  *How:* One visually unique agent: a warm white-gold sprite (e.g. #FFC46B) with a trailing ribbon, the only warm object in a cool palette. Arc: alone and capable (Act I) → racing, then trapped under queues (Act II) → one of thousands, in its place on the line (Act III) → alone again in the opening composition, now with the fleet igniting behind it (button). Give it agency: it moves first and orients ('looks') before acting. Tie it to a 4–6 note melodic motif: sparse in Act I, minor or inverted in Act II, full hybrid statement in Act III and on the end title.
  *Reference:* Orlando Wood's right-brain features (a character, a place, a story, melody). Pixar's lamp: a character can be a light.
- **Peak-end engineering plus button**. *When:* The last 20–25 s
  *How:* Biggest audiovisual peak at 80–88% of runtime (about 1:50–1:56 of 2:11): alternating angles, 6 f shots, braam #3. Then a false stop (15 f black and silence), an end title of 5 s or less, and a 4–7 s button: a callback that recasts the whole piece ('MONTH 1.' over the lone spark as thousands ignite). Final hit, ring-out 2.5 s or less. No recap, no summary cards, no slow fade-down, and the music must not resolve before the end title.
  *Reference:* Lieu's 'button' (a quick snippet after the end title). Wistia: recaps and sign-offs cause drop-off before the end. Kahneman's peak-end rule.
- **Cut into motion, cut out of motion (removing the 'slow' tells)**. *When:* Every shot. This is the direct fix for the first version's slowness.
  *How:* Each shot's camera starts at 60% or more of cruising speed and is cut before decelerating below 50%. Replace long easeInOut moves with linear or easeOutQuart. Replace crossfades with hard cuts: at most 2 dissolves in the film, each 12 f or less. Speed ramp: 1× → 0.2× for 8–12 f on impact → a 3–4× whip, with 8–16 subframes of accumulation motion blur (equivalent to a 360° shutter in the whip). Whip-pan transitions: 6–8 f, peak about 600–900°/s, cut hidden at peak blur.
  *Reference:* Fury Road's frame-rate manipulation and speed ramps; Apple product films' speed-ramped macro light sweeps.
- **Proof-shot rule for executive credibility**. *When:* Every claim card, especially after the product reveal
  *How:* Every claim gets a visual proof within ±2 s that shows the mechanism, never a stock 'AI brain'. At most one technical noun per card. Every number carries a unit and has a source, shown as a 1 s source tag at 18–22 px. After the reveal, 3–4 capability cards at most (4 words or fewer each), each with its proof shot; a spec dump kills momentum. Kill-list: revolutionary, unleash, supercharge, game-changing, 'the future of', exclamation marks.
  *Reference:* Apple keynote films: short claim, big number, footnote. The Social Network teaser (2010): UI-as-world imagery, restrained copy, and a choral 'Creep' cover giving gravitas without hype.
- **Integer-frame tempo grid with a tempo lift**. *When:* Before composing any audio; the master clock for picture and sound
  *How:* At 30 fps, pick BPMs that give whole frames per beat: 90 (20 f), 100 (18 f), 120 (15 f), 150 (12 f; 8th = 6 f, 16th = 3 f), 180 (10 f). Acts I–II at 120 BPM (bar = 60 f = 2 s; 4-bar phrase = 8 s). Act III lifts to 150 BPM (bar = 48 f); the tempo lift is felt as escalation. Every cut, card and hit time is computed from one timeline: beats → frames → samples (×1600 at 48 kHz). That makes cuts sample-accurate to hits.
  *Reference:* Trailer music's three-act build: sparse pulse → percussion build with risers → full hybrid with braams, as in the Inception-era trailers.

### Template / numeric rules / recipes

A) NUMERIC RULES (all frames at 30 fps)
- Runtime: 2:11 core (3930 f), plus an optional 4 s CTA, giving 2:15 at most. Hard ceiling 2:30.
- Cuts: about 100–120 shots in total. ASL by act: cold open 1.0–1.5 s | Act I 1.6–2.2 s | Act II 1.0–1.5 s | Act III 0.5–0.9 s | peak bar 6–10 f. Content shots never under 4 f.
- Change events: something changes at least every 2.0 s (60 f) outside designated holds. A change event is any of: a cut; a camera velocity or direction change over 30%; a new text element; an audio onset at least 6 dB above the bed within 50 ms; a mean-luminance change of 20% or more. A major interrupt (new scene, scale, palette or sonic register) every 5–8 s. A re-hook (new question or payoff) every 15–20 s. At most 4 designated holds, each 2.5–4.0 s, each with 2–4% drift.
- Text per card: 7 words or fewer and 42 characters or fewer (Netflix's line limit); median 4 words or fewer. ALL CAPS only for 4 words or fewer (all-caps text reads more slowly); sentence case for 5–7 words.
- Text hold times (the clock starts when the last word lands). Key claim: frames = 12 + 2 × characters (about 15 cps plus 0.4 s to orient). Support card: frames = 9 + 1.5 × characters (20 cps, Netflix's adult limit). Floors: 15 f for any card of 2 or more words; 8 f for a single short predictable word. Example: 'SHIPPING DIDN'T.' (16 chars) is 44 f as a key claim, 33 f as support. Cards should break whenever the formula exceeds about 60 f.
- Text budget: 100–140 on-screen words in total (about 50–65 words per minute averaged over the runtime). Text on screen 40% of runtime or less; pure image 60% or more.
- Type: cap height 72–140 px at 1080p for must-read words (60 px minimum). Keep text inside the centre 80% safe area so 1:1 and 9:16 crops survive. Contrast 7:1 or better. Entrance 3–5 f with easeOutExpo, cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1). Exit by hard cut.
- Loudness (web): integrated -14 LUFS, true peak -1 dBTP or lower. Short-term (3 s) targets: Act I about -20, Act II about -17 rising to -14, Act III -12 to -10 at peak. Silence drops: momentary loudness of -50 LUFS or lower for 12–30 f. Braams 3–6 LU above their surroundings.
- Tempo: 120 BPM (15 f per beat) until 1:14, then 150 BPM (12 f per beat). Every cut and card lands within ±1 f of a beat or a hit.
- Effects budget: braams 3 | silence drops 3 | flashes 10 or fewer (never more than 3 in any 1 s window) | glitches 6 or fewer (Act II only) | shakes 8 or fewer | whips 6 or fewer | dissolves 2 or fewer.

 T4 No more than 3 cards stacked without an image beat of at least 8 f.
 T5 While a key card is up, background motion is 5% of frame width per second or less and contrast is 7:1 or better.
 T6 Every claim card has a proof shot within ±2 s; every number has a unit and a source.
 T7 Zero hype words from the kill-list; zero exclamation marks.
STORY
 S1 The beat list has zero AND THEN links (every link is BUT or THEREFORE).
 S2 At least 3 loops opened; each closes within 70 s; each closure opens the next.
 S3 The protagonist spark appears in at least 6 beats and changes state at least 3 times.
 S4 The problem act is 25–35% of runtime and escalates in at least 3 distinct steps.
 S5 The mirror callback is present: at least 4 Act II compositions return transformed in Act III.
 S6 A melodic motif appears in at least 3 acts.
ESCALATION AND DYNAMICS
 E1 Short-term LUFS by act rises monotonically (Act I < Act II < Act III), with at least 8 LU between the quietest non-silent passage and the peak.
 E2 Exactly 3 silence drops, each 12–30 f at -50 LUFS momentary or lower; none longer than 1.5 s.
 E3 The visible agent count rises each act (1 → hundreds → thousands or more).
 E4 At least 6 distinct angle classes used; no class 3 times in a row.
 E5 The global peak (loudness, cut rate and brightness together) sits at 80–88% of runtime.
HABITUATION AND SAFETY
 B1 No identical SFX twice in a row; every repeat varies pitch, filter or layer.
 B2 The effects budget in A is respected; no more than 3 flashes in any 1 s window; no saturated-red full-frame flashes.
ENDING
 N1 No recap or summary; the music does not resolve before the end title.
 N2 End title 5 s or less; button 4–7 s; ring-out 2.5 s or less; core runtime 2:15 or less.

D) AUTOMATED AUDIT (the film is generated in code, so audit the timeline itself)
- Emit timeline.json as the single source of truth: shots [{start_f, end_f, act, angle_class, focal_xy, is_hold}], cards [{start_f, last_word_f, end_f, text, is_key}], sfx [{f, family, pitch_st, gain_db}], fx [{f, type: flash|shake|glitch|whip|dissolve}], loops [{id, open_f, close_f}], links [{from_beat, to_beat, type: BUT|THEREFORE|ANDTHEN}].
- The script computes C1–C5 (gaps between change events, ASL and coefficient of variation per act, beat-grid deviation), T1–T4 (words, characters, cps, coverage), S1–S2, E4, B1–B2 and N2, and fails the build on any violation.
- Render checks: decode the frames. Mean luminance per frame gives flash detection (a jump over 20% of full range counts as a flash; at most 3 per 30 f window). A frame-difference histogram finds cuts and checks them against the timeline. A 1 fps contact sheet: adjacent-second similarity (SSIM) should stay below 0.6 for at least 85% of pairs outside holds.
- Audio checks with pyloudnorm: integrated loudness and true peak; a 3 s short-term loudness curve per act; 400 ms momentary loudness for the silence windows; check that no two consecutive SFX share the same (family, pitch).

E) HUMAN TESTS (5 viewers who have not seen it, ideally engineers or managers)
- 10 s stop test: 'What is this about?' Pass at 4 of 5.
- Boredom-flag test: viewers tap a key whenever their attention drifts. Any 3 s window flagged by 2 or more viewers gets recut.
- Recall 60 s after viewing: the problem, the 'next wave' idea and the product name at 4 of 5; the '5 months' number at 3 of 5.
- One-sentence retell (Lieu: can people pass it on?): at least 3 of 5 retell the same story.
- Muted pass: the story still follows. Eyes-closed pass: the act boundaries and the energy curve are audible.

### Pitfalls
- Opening on a logo, title, black frame, fade-in or slow push from rest. Each of these spends the most valuable 3 s on nothing; Wistia saw early viewer loss halve once a long intro was cut to under 10 s.
- Crossfades and eased moves everywhere: the clearest sign of 'slow'. Each 0.5–1 s dissolve removes energy. Use hard cuts, cut into motion, and keep at most 2 dissolves.
- Dead holds: a card stays up 1–2 s after it has been read, or a spectacle shot lingers after the reveal has registered. Cut on comprehension plus 4–8 f.
- A metronome edit: equal card durations and equal shot lengths. Fast but uniform still feels monotonous; vary lengths (coefficient of variation 0.4–0.8) and use holds deliberately.
- Text over busy, moving 3D. Two focal points compete and the viewer reads neither. Apply the read-or-watch rule.
- Too many words: more than 7 words per card, more than 20 cps, long ALL-CAPS sentences, or more than about 140 words in total. A no-voiceover film turns into a slideshow.
- Problem as a list, not an escalation. The client wants the problem dwelt on, but stating complaints one after another (AND THEN) bores. It needs 3 escalating steps with a ticking clock and a low point.
- Effects spam: a braam, flash, shake or glitch on every cut. The effects habituate (Potter et al.), read as cheap hype to executives, and flashing faster than 3 per second is a photosensitive-epilepsy risk (WCAG 2.3.1).
- Reusing the identical hit or whoosh sample, or music looping unchanged for more than 4–8 bars. The orienting response decays; vary pitch, filter and layer, and change something every 4 bars.
- Revealing the product before the problem peaks, or following the reveal with a spec list. Reveal at about 70–76% of runtime, then 3–4 proof-backed claims at most.
- Hype vocabulary and unsourced superlatives ('revolutionary', 'unleash', 'the future of'), or numbers without units or sources. Technical executives drop out of trust faster than out of attention. The '5 months' claim and every statistic must be verified, not invented.
- Ending badly: a recap, a long end card over 6 s, a slow fade-down, or the music resolving before the title. All of these signal 'you can leave now' (Wistia's late drop-off).
- Grand but murky: an all-dark, fog-heavy palette that turns to mud on phones. Give the key subject high luminance, include a high-key frame at least every 10 s, and keep text contrast at 7:1 or better.
- Silences longer than 1.5 s, which read as a broken video in social autoplay. Keep drops to 12–30 f.
- A tempo grid that doesn't land on whole frames (for example 128 or 140 BPM at 30 fps). Cuts drift up to half a frame off the hits and accumulate error. Use 90, 100, 120, 150 or 180 BPM, and derive frames and samples from one master beat clock.
- A hook that depends on sound. Muted autoplay is common on LinkedIn and X, so the first 3 s must work silently.
- A wholly abstract film: words plus particles, with no character and no place. This is the 'left-brain' failure Wood describes. Give it the protagonist spark, the physical factory and a melodic motif.
- Fast cuts between unrelated shots. LC4MP predicts they cost memory; keep montages semantically related (same motif, protagonist or palette).
- Treating creator-blog rules ('a pattern interrupt every 2 s') as research. Interval advice in creator guides ranges from about 2 s to 45 s and no platform mandates any interval (Creator Essentials). The numbers here are calibrated for a 2:15 trailer and should be validated with the boredom-flag test.

### Principles
- DARKNESS IS THE CANVAS, LIGHT IS THE SUBJECT. 60–85% of every frame should be near-black (luma under 5%). Grandeur comes from a few HDR-bright things inside a large, foggy, layered dark volume. Flat mid-gray backgrounds read as corporate.
- ONE HOT ACCENT, RATIONED LIKE A STORY BUDGET. Claude clay #D97757 (Anthropic's primary accent) covers 2% or less of frame area through the problem acts. It appears only on meaning (the agent, the fix, the title) and expands to 10–20% at the reveal. Apple's rule from the sources: plain black or white ground with one saturated color on the subject, never bright on bright. Nothing's version: a very small pop of red is enough.
- COLOR TEMPERATURE IS THE PLOT. Problem acts are cold and desaturated (steel #5B7A93, ice #CFE3F2, alert red only as blinking pinpoints, saturation 0.8). The turn is black and silent. The solution is warm (clay, ember, brass metal, ivory #FAF9F5 type, saturation 1.05). Viewers should feel the turn before they read it.
- ONLY TWO TYPE SCALES: MONUMENT AND WHISPER. Monument means one word fills 70–100% of frame width, and letters may bleed off-frame. Whisper means 22–30px tracked mono caps in a sea of black. Mid-size centered sentences (60–100px) on flat black with fades are the 'deck zone'. That is almost certainly why v1 felt slow, so avoid it.
- TYPE IS AN IMAGE, NOT A CAPTION. Every card must also be a visual event. It is lit in 3D space, cut out of light, assembled from particles, stamped on a hit, or used as architecture the camera flies past. Never fade text in over a pad.
- ANIMATE IN, DRIFT WHILE HELD, HARD-CUT OUT. Arrivals use expo-out. Nothing is ever static: keep a continuous 1–3% push or drift. Exits are cuts on the beat, not fades. The whole film gets at most one cross-dissolve, used deliberately to show time passing.
- THE CONTRAST ENGINE KEEPS EYES LOCKED. Every cut must change at least two of: scale (macro or mega), angle (top, bottom or eye level), direction of motion, brightness, color temperature, and medium (type or 3D). Shot lengths must vary from 4 frames to 4 seconds; uniform pacing feels monotonous.
- CENTER-WEIGHTED FRAMING FOR SPEED. In shots shorter than 15 frames, keep the focal point inside the central 30% and match focal points across cuts (eye trace). Mad Max: Fury Road stayed readable even at 12× speed because of crosshair framing.
- SCALE NEEDS A REFERENCE. Set a tiny element (one glowing agent node, one cursor) against huge architecture, with 3–4 fog depth layers (atmospheric perspective) and a low camera. Size without a reference reads as nothing. Blade Runner 2049's production-design brief was 'brutality, scale and power'.
- RESTRAINT SIGNALS EXPENSE. GMUNK called Tron Legacy's UI 'tame and tasteful' and stripped out greeble for legibility. Apple uses one shot per idea with breathing room. Flare, chromatic aberration (CA), glitch, shake and flash are punctuation tied to musical hits, never wallpaper.
- LIGHT MUST BEHAVE PHYSICALLY. Use HDR emissives (values 4–20), threshold bloom, and AgX/ACES tone mapping to get real highlight rolloff. Holograms and beams should light nearby surfaces, as Tron's holograms cast light on the actors. CSS outer-glow on everything reads as a template.
- PRECISION IS PREMIUM. Use 1px hairlines, tabular figures, one grid (96px margins, 12 columns), supersampled anti-aliasing, frame-exact music sync, and one type system across titles, labels and UI. Aliasing, banding, jitter or a fourth font reads cheap instantly.
- A SMALL MOTIF SYSTEM CARRIES THE STORY WITHOUT VO. Choose three recurring motifs, for example the cursor block ▮, the horizontal light line, and the node or flow path. Evolve them across acts: cursor → city of cursors → factory tower. Repetition with escalation is visual storytelling.
- TEXTURE BEATS STERILITY. Use deterministic animated grain, slight halation and vignette. This echoes Anthropic's 'Keep thinking' campaign, shot on physical film for human texture. It also dithers dark gradients so they don't band.
- BLACK AND SILENCE ARE SHOTS TOO. A 12–24 frame smash to pure black before the turn and before the title gives the following hit its size. The Keep thinking spot marks its own turn with a pause in the montage.

### Techniques
- **Slam-in (scale-down-from-giant impact)**. *When:* Problem-barrage words, stat numbers, and act-ending hits on downbeats. At most about 1 every 2 seconds, or it stops being an event.
  *How:* Text is Inter Tight 900 or Archivo Black, uppercase, tracking -0.04em, cap height 35–45% of frame height. Frame 0: scale 3.2, opacity 0, zoom blur 40px. Frames 0–5: scale → 1.0 with expo-out cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1), opacity reaching 1 by f2, blur → 0. IMPACT at f5, exactly on the beat (start the move 5 frames before the beat). (a) Flash: exposure +1.5 stops for 1 frame (renderer.toneMappingExposure ×2.8) or a 40% white overlay limited to the type region. (b) Screen shake: amplitude 14px × exp(-f/3) over 10 frames, with a direction from a seeded RNG each frame. (c) Radial CA spike: offset 0.006 → 0.0008 over 8 frames. (d) Background dust or particles burst outward at 0.3 units/frame, decaying. HOLD: scale 1.00 → 1.035 linear until a hard cut. Never animate it out.
  *Reference:* Apple chip-event stat slams ('3nm'), NVIDIA GTC keynote openers. The trailer convention is hit plus flash plus card on the downbeat.
- **Mask-rise line reveal (readable story card)**. *When:* 3–8 word narrative cards that must actually be read, such as the story beats of the coding-agent wave and the factory.
  *How:* Put each word in an overflow:hidden span. translateY(110%) → 0 over 12 frames, cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1), with an optional rotate 3° → 0. Word stagger is 2–3 frames. Hold = 0.25 s per word + 0.6 s (5 words ≈ 56 frames). Exit is a hard cut. Only when the next card shares the same background, use a mask-drop out: translateY 0 → -110% over 8 frames, expo-in cubic-bezier(0.7,0,0.84,0). At most 2 lines. Size 120–180px headline, or Monument for 1–2 words.
  *Reference:* Apple product-film headlines: copy fades, scales or slides into place but never competes with the message. Linear launch films.
- **Blur-to-sharp tracking settle (the Nolan/Dune title card)**. *When:* Emotional lines, the turn question ('What if the whole line ran itself?'), and the end title.
  *How:* Thin weight (Inter Tight 300) uppercase, 72–110px, ivory #FAF9F5. Over 24 frames: filter blur 28px → 0 (easeOutCubic), letter-spacing +0.45em → +0.08em (easeOutQuart), opacity 0 → 1 in the first 10 frames, scale 1.06 → 1.0. During the hold, tracking breathes +0.08em → +0.14em linear over 3 s. Put it on pure black, or over a defocused warm light at 10–20% luminance.
  *Reference:* Interstellar, Dune and Tenet trailer cards: widely tracked thin caps that resolve out of blur.
- **Per-letter cascade (3D flip-up)**. *When:* One important word, such as FACTORY or the product name. Use it 2–3 times in the film at most.
  *How:* Per-letter duration is 10 frames. Stagger s = min(2, 16/n) frames, so the total stays under 26 frames. Each letter goes y +60% → 0, opacity 0 → 1, blur 10px → 0, rotateX 70° → 0 (perspective 800px), expo-out. Use center-out order for a monumental symmetric reveal, left-to-right when reading matters. Land the last letter on the beat.
  *Reference:* The symmetry obsession in Westworld's titles (Patrick Clair: 'circles and squares and symmetry and balance'). Apple 'Pro' wordmark reveals.
- **Decode / scramble (cinematic UI caption)**. *When:* Agent and data moments, station labels, file names, whisper captions. Use at most 1 per 10 seconds or it becomes a gimmick.
  *How:* JetBrains Mono 500, 22–30px, caps, +0.15em tracking. Each unlocked char cycles a random glyph from 'ABCDEF0123456789#/<>{}' every frame, from an RNG seeded with (frame, index). Char i locks at frame 4 + i. Locked chars are ivory; unlocked chars are clay at 60% opacity. Optional lead-in: a cursor block ▮ blinks twice with an 8-frame period.
  *Reference:* Territory Studio's story-specific screens (Blade Runner 2049), Tron Legacy interfaces, Bungie's Marathon reveal and its graphic-design UI.
- **Word-swap barrage on a fixed baseline**. *When:* The problem montage: the pile-up listed as TICKETS / REVIEWS / FLAKY TESTS / ROLLBACKS / ON-CALL / 3 A.M.
  *How:* Lock position and size at center. Swap the word every 1/8 note (8 frames at 112.5 BPM), switching to 1/16 notes (4 frames) in the last bar. Behind each swap, alternate 3D vignettes: top-down jammed conveyor, low-angle red-lit pile, then pure black. Every 4th word is a slam-in. Keep full-field luminance swings to 3 or fewer per second: the vignettes stay dark, and the bright content is under 25% of the area.
  *Reference:* Enter the Void's strobing opening credits (adapt at lower flash intensity). NVIDIA 'I AM AI' anaphora litany, done as type instead of VO.
- **Repetition wall / speed-ramped marquee**. *When:* Visualizing overload ('the backlog grows faster than humans can clear it') just before the smash to black.
  *How:* 9–14 rows of the same words at 120–180px. Alternate row directions at 300–900 px/s. Rows sit at 15–40% opacity, with one row at 100% (the alert or accent). Optionally put the wall on a 3D plane tilted 25–35° into fog. Speed multiplier ramps ×1 → ×4 over 60 frames (expo-in), and shake builds 0 → 8px. Then hard cut to 100% black plus silence for 18–24 frames.
  *Reference:* Fashion and Nike-style kinetic openers. Use it as the 'noise' you cut out of right before the turn.
- **Type as architecture (monument letters)**. *When:* The solution reveal, for example 'SHIP' or 'FACTORY' as buildings on the line, filmed from a bottom angle.
  *How:* Extruded TextGeometry: depth 0.3× size, tiny bevel 0.005 or none. MeshPhysicalMaterial: metalness 1, roughness 0.22, clearcoat 1, anisotropy 0.6 for a brushed look. Environment: black with 2–3 long emissive strip planes (intensity 8–15) baked to PMREM. Camera: height 0.3 units, FOV 60°, pitch +15°, dolly forward 2–4% per second. Fog: FogExp2 density 0.035, color #0B0B0C. Behind the letters, additive volumetric cones (opacity 0.06–0.12, scrolling noise texture). Run a 0.9 s strip-light sweep so a specular band crosses the letters.
  *Reference:* Blade Runner 2049's monumental low angles, Apple's metal hero shots, the Westworld title look.
- **Light-line forge (title or logo reveal)**. *When:* Main title ('THE SOFTWARE FACTORY') and the end lockup ('Claude Opus 5.5').
  *How:* f0–12: black, sub only. f12–20: a 2px HDR line (emissive 12, clay → ivory core) grows from center to 70% width, expo-out, with an anamorphic streak (horizontal-only blur about 300px, tinted clay, 0.4 opacity). f20–28: the line splits into two lines moving apart vertically, and the title is revealed in the slot between them (clip-path inset bound to the line y positions). f28: HIT, plus a 1-frame flash covering 40% or less of the screen. f28–48: metallic sweep across the type (gradient band 18% wide, angle 24°, travelling -30% → 130%; fill gradient #FFFFFF / #E9E4DA 45% / #9C8F80 50% / #F4EEE4 55% / #FFFFFF). Hold 60–90 frames with a 2% push-in while embers drift up 0.02 units per frame. Hard cut to black on the final music button.
  *Reference:* Dune and Interstellar title austerity, the Apple logo light-sweep, the beams-through-fog hero on Linear's site.
- **Particle assemble / derezz**. *When:* Chaos → order transitions: the factory silhouette or title assembling from data, or the old workflow dissolving.
  *How:* Draw the target shape or text to an offscreen canvas and sample every 2–3px where alpha > 0.5, giving 40–120k points. Start positions come from a seeded noise sphere. pos = mix(start, target, easeOutExpo(clamp((t - d_i)/0.9))), with d_i = 0.35·x_norm for a left-to-right sweep. Add curl-noise displacement × (1 - progress). Point size 1.5–3px, additive blending, HDR color 3–6 so bloom catches it. Snap and flash on the final 4 frames. For derezz, run it in reverse with expo-in and stretch the points along velocity.
  *Reference:* Tron Legacy derezz and disc-hologram particle rendering. NVIDIA keynote particle intros.
- **Wireframe-to-solid build**. *When:* 'The plan becomes the product': factory stations assembling along the line.
  *How:* Phase 1: EdgesGeometry lines (opacity 0.35, ivory or clay) drawn by growing drawRange over 10–16 frames. Phase 2, on the beat: the solid physical material is revealed by a clipping plane rising 1 unit over 12 frames, with a thin emissive ring (emissive 8) at the clip edge as a scan line. Stagger objects 3–4 frames down the conveyor so the build travels like a wave.
  *Reference:* Westworld's 3D-printer construction of bodies (macro 'being-built' shots), Iron Man fabrication HUDs.
- **Light-trail data streams (agent fleets)**. *When:* Work flowing through the line: agents carrying tasks between stations, and speed sections.
  *How:* InstancedMesh of thin boxes (0.01×0.01×L), 300–3000 instances, oriented along CatmullRomCurve3 paths. Streak length L = 0.2 + 0.15·speed (stretching with velocity sells motion blur). HDR emissive 6–15, clay for agents and ivory for tasks. Time-remap speed ramp: 1× → 0.25× slow-mo for 12–20 frames at an impact → 3×. Optional AfterimagePass with damp 0.85–0.92 for long trails in a single hero shot.
  *Reference:* Tron light-cycle trails (the visual language, not GMUNK's unit). Data-highway shots in chip-launch films.
- **Glass gates with dispersion (review and eval gates)**. *When:* Visualizing checks, reviews and tests as premium physical layers that work passes through.
  *How:* MeshPhysicalMaterial: transmission 1, thickness 0.6–1.5, ior 1.5, roughness 0.08, dispersion 0.2–0.6 (three r163+), attenuationColor clay, attenuationDistance 1.5. Hard rim backlight: DirectionalLight 4–6 behind, 30° above. Work items change from cold to warm emissive as they exit a gate.
  *Reference:* The glass logo with fog beams on Linear's site, Apple Vision Pro glass UI films.
- **Top angle / bottom angle / macro triad**. *When:* The whole film, and especially Acts II–III. This is the client's 'top angle, bottom angle' request.
  *How:* TOP: pitch -90°, FOV 18–25° (telephoto flattening gives a graphic map), rotate around Y at 4–8°/s. It reads the system and its scale. BOTTOM: height 0.2–0.5 units, FOV 60–72°, pitch +10–25°, dolly-in 2–4% per second, subject in the upper two-thirds. It reads monumental. MACRO: FOV 12–18°, DOF bokehScale 2–4, focus pulled across 10 frames. It reads detail and intimacy. Cut between them on downbeats, pairing top = factory flow with bottom = one hero station or agent.
  *Reference:* Blade Runner 2049 (overhead spinner vs. low monuments). Apple M-series chip reveals (top-down die → low orbit with light racing along traces).
- **Crash zoom and whip-pan transitions (no dissolves)**. *When:* Linking shots inside fast montages.
  *How:* Crash zoom: FOV 55 → 18 over 5 frames, expo-in, plus 6px shake. Whip: yaw 70° over 7 frames, easeInOutCubic, with directional blur (accumulation of 12 subframes, or a 60px shader blur). Cut at frame 4, the blur peak, into shot B, which starts mid-whip and decelerates over 5 frames. Optional shape match: put the same dominant shape at the same screen position across the cut.
  *Reference:* Apple keynote product changeovers (the camera 'practically never stands still' and transitions replace plain cuts). Game trailers.
- **Flash / exposure cut**. *When:* Downbeat hits and act turns.
  *How:* 1–2 frames at +2 stops (toneMappingExposure ×4), or a clay-tinted overlay at 60–80%, then exposure decays back over 4–6 frames exponentially. Limits: 3 per second at most, localized (25% of the area or less) by default, and full-field flashes no more than about 6 in the whole film. Never alternate saturated red full-field.
  *Reference:* Classic trailer flash frames between shots (Inception and Dark Knight era).
- **Letterbox breathing**. *When:* Acts I–II at tight 2.39:1 for claustrophobia. Open to full 1.78:1 at the solution reveal, so the frame literally opens up.
  *How:* At 1920×1080, 2.39:1 leaves an 803px image with bars of 138–139px each; 2.0:1 gives 60px bars. Snap the bars open on the reveal hit frame, or animate them over 24–36 frames with cubic-bezier(0.65,0,0.35,1). Bars are pure #000000 even if the grade lifts blacks. Type stays inside the inner 90% of the image area and never touches a bar.
  *Reference:* Nolan's IMAX aspect-ratio expansions (The Dark Knight, Interstellar).
- **Strip-light reflections (the Apple sheen)**. *When:* Any glossy hero object, such as the factory core or the Opus 5.5 wordmark slab.
  *How:* Black environment with 2–3 long rectangular emissive softboxes (intensity 8–15) via RectAreaLight, or emissive planes baked to PMREM. Move the LIGHTS slowly so reflections glide across the surface while the object stays nearly still. Rim light 3–5× key from behind, fill near 0. Material: roughness 0.15–0.3, clearcoat 1, anisotropy 0.6 for brushed metal.
  *Reference:* Apple iPhone 15 Pro 'Titanium' and MacBook Pro hero films: one saturated or metallic subject on black, with reflections that move.
- **Film finish stack (grain, CA, vignette, halation)**. *When:* Always on, subtly. Spike CA and grain on hits.
  *How:* Grain: Gaussian noise seeded by frame index, σ 0.035–0.05 in display space, about 1.2px, weighted by a midtone mask 4·L·(1-L) so blacks stay black. CA: radial, offset 0.0005 (about 1px at the edges) at baseline, spiking to 0.005 for 6 frames on hits. Vignette: offset 0.35, darkness 0.5. Halation: threshold highlights at 0.9, blur 20px, add back tinted #FF6A3D at 0.08. All of it goes AFTER tone mapping and grade so the grain isn't graded.
  *Reference:* Anthropic 'Keep thinking' (shot on Ektachrome, 16mm and 35mm for human texture), Blade Runner 2049 haze.
- **Counter / stat slam with tabular figures**. *When:* The '~5 months from capability to budget' beat and any verified numeric claim.
  *How:* Use font-feature-settings 'tnum'. Count the value over 20–24 frames with expo-out (value = round(target × ease)). The final digit locks on the beat with a slam impact. Unit label in small mono caps (+0.2em) beneath it. Pair it with a timeline bar filling 0 → 100% in sync, with segment ticks at 1/16 notes.
  *Reference:* Apple event spec slams, NVIDIA keynote numbers.
- **Shape match cut (story without words)**. *When:* Bridging acts and evolving motifs.
  *How:* Shot A ends with a dominant shape at center (cursor block, circle node or horizon line). Shot B starts with the same shape at the same screen position and a size within ±10%, but a different scale or meaning: cursor block → monolith tower, node → factory core, light line → horizon. Cut on the beat.
  *Reference:* The bone-to-satellite cut in 2001. In Westworld, the player-piano roll becomes the built body.
- **Animate machines on twos**. *When:* Robotic arms, agents and stamping presses, to make them feel precise and stylized against a smooth camera.
  *How:* Quantize object time to t_obj = floor(f/2)·2/30 while the camera moves on every frame. This gives crisp mechanical stepping. Switch to ones in slow-mo moments for contrast.
  *Reference:* Basement.studio's Vercel Ship 2025 opener animated characters on twos (12 fps poses inside 24 fps camera motion) for a stylized rhythm.

A. STYLE BIBLE (tokens to hard-code)

- PALETTE:
  - VOID #050506. Letterbox bars are pure #000.
  - SLATE #141413 (Anthropic dark), GRAPHITE #262624, ASH #6E6C66, MID #B0AEA5.
  - IVORY #FAF9F5: all primary type.
  - CLAY #D97757: the single hot accent. EMBER #FF8A5C is its HDR emissive tone.
  - BRASS metal gradient #F3D9B1 → #B88A5A → #4A3626.
  - COLD SET (problem acts only): STEEL #5B7A93, ICE #CFE3F2, ALERT #FF453A (blinking pinpoints only, 1% of area or less).
  - Area ratios, Acts I–II: 85% void/slate, 12% cold grays, 2% ivory, ≤1% alert/clay.
  - Area ratios, Acts III–IV: 75% void, 15% warm metal/ivory, 5–10% clay.
  - Final lockup: clay up to 20%.
- TYPE (3 families max):
  - Display: Inter Tight (OFL), weights 300 and 900. Tracking -0.04em at 200px or more, -0.02em at 80–200px. Line-height 0.86–0.92.
  - Condensed slam alternative: Archivo, wdth 62, wght 900. Animating wdth 62 → 125 over 6 frames on a hit is a strong effect.
  - Labels: JetBrains Mono 500, caps, +0.15–0.2em, 22–30px.
  - Optional emotional accent: Instrument Serif Italic, one word per card at most.
  - SIZES: Monument 360–620px (or fit to 88–100% of width), Headline 120–180px, Line 64–88px (rare), Whisper 22–30px.
  - READ TIMES: 1 word 10–15f (minimum 8f). 2–4 words 30–45f. 5–8 words 1.6–2.4 s. Never more than 8 words or 2 lines per card.
- EASING:
  - ARRIVE: cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)
  - SETTLE: (0.25,1,0.5,1)
  - LEAVE / accelerate into a cut: (0.7,0,0.84,0)
  - CAMERA: (0.65,0,0.35,1)
  - HOLD-DRIFT: linear
  - No bounce or overshoot on display type. If you use springs, make them critically damped (damping ≈ 2√k).
- MUSIC GRID: frames per beat = 1800/BPM. Choose a BPM that gives integer frames.
  - 112.5 BPM: beat 16f, 1/8 = 8f, 1/16 = 4f, bar 64f (2.133 s). Recommended.
  - 90 BPM: 20f / 10f / 5f.
  - Type and camera moves START 4–5 frames before the beat so the impact frame lands on it. Cuts land on beats.
- SHOT LENGTHS:
  - Act I: 40–60f.
  - Barrage: 8–16f, compressing to 4f in the last bar.
  - Turn: 24–60f of black or a single line.
  - Act III: 30–75f, always with internal motion.
  - Montage: 8–16f.
  - Title: 90–150f. Stinger: 60–120f.
  - Overall average shot length (ASL) 1.2–1.8 s. Nothing static for more than 20 frames.

C. THREE.JS POST STACK (pmndrs postprocessing)
- Renderer: WebGLRenderer({antialias:false, preserveDrawingBuffer:true}), outputColorSpace = SRGBColorSpace.
  - Renderer toneMapping = NoToneMapping when using ToneMappingEffect; never tone-map twice.
  - EffectComposer: frameBufferType HalfFloatType, multisampling 4.
- ORDER:
  1. RenderPass.
  2. DepthOfFieldEffect (macro shots only, bokehScale 2–4).
  3. BloomEffect {mipmapBlur:true, luminanceThreshold 0.9, luminanceSmoothing 0.15, intensity 0.9 (1.8 on hits), radius 0.75}.
  4. GodRaysEffect, beam shots only {samples 60, density 0.96, decay 0.93, weight 0.4, exposure 0.55}.
  5. ChromaticAberrationEffect {offset (0.0005,0.0003), radialModulation:true, modulationOffset 0.25}.
  6. ToneMappingEffect {mode AGX, or ACES_FILMIC}.
  7. Grade: LUT3DEffect or a custom lift/gamma/gain pass (lift -0.01, gain 1.05; saturation 0.8 cold / 1.05 warm; hue-protect clay).
  8. VignetteEffect {offset 0.35, darkness 0.5}.
  9. Custom grain with frame-seeded uniform. pmndrs NoiseEffect is time-driven, so replace or seed it.
  10. Letterbox pass.
- Emissives: give bloom sources emissiveIntensity 4–20. Keep type that must be read at emissive 1.0–1.5 so it doesn't halo into mush, or bloom it in a separate layer at 0.3 or less.
- Fog: FogExp2 0.02–0.06. Build 3–4 depth layers of geometry for atmospheric perspective. Dust motes: 1–2k points, 1–3px, drifting 0.02 units/frame, visible only inside beams.
- Infinite grid: shader with fwidth anti-aliasing, fading by distance (alpha = 1 - smoothstep(20, 80, dist)), major lines every 10 units at 2× brightness, and occasional clay pulses travelling along lines at 30 units/s.
- MOTION BLUR (fast shots only): accumulate N = 8–16 subframes at times f/30 + (k/N)·(0.5/30) (180° shutter), averaged in linear float.
- DETERMINISM: drive everything from frame index. GSAP timeline paused and .seek(f/30). Seeded RNG (mulberry32(seed + f)). No Math.random, no performance.now.
- TYPE LAYER:
  - HTML/SVG overlay composited in the same page, captured by Puppeteer per frame.
  - Text that must be lit or bloomed goes in WebGL via troika-three-text (SDF) or TextGeometry.
  - SVG circuit traces: stroke-dashoffset draw over 12–20f (easeInOutCubic), with an feGaussianBlur stdDeviation 3 glow merge.
  - Metallic fill: background-clip:text with the brass or chrome gradient. Light sweep: an animated mask-image linear-gradient band.
- ENCODE: ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i f_%05d.png -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 14 -tune grain -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart. Keep a 10-bit ProRes or HEVC master. Inspect dark fog gradients for banding before sign-off.

### Pitfalls
- THE V1 FAILURE MODE: mid-size centered sentences on flat black with fade-in and fade-out over a pad. That's a slide deck. Replace it with Monument/Whisper type, hard cuts and visual events.
- Cross-dissolves and fade-outs drain energy. Use hard cuts on the beat, flash cuts, smash-to-black, match cuts and whip transitions.
- Uniform pacing, with every shot about 2 s and every move the same ease, feels like a screensaver. Vary shots from 4 frames to 4 seconds and alternate locked-off graphic frames with aggressive moves.
- Static holds: anything frozen for more than 20 frames looks dead. Always keep 1–3% drift or push.
- Effects as wallpaper. Constant shake, CA, glitch and lens flares (the JJ Abrams trap) cause fatigue and read as stock templates. Tie every effect to a hit, and use glitch at most once every 10 s.
- Bloom threshold too low (under 0.7) makes the whole frame milky and type halos become unreadable. Bloom only HDR emissives, and keep readable type out of strong bloom.
- Too many colors or fonts: rainbow or iridescent gradients everywhere, multiple accents, a 4th font family, bevel/emboss/drop shadows, clichéd 'futuristic' faces (Orbitron, Bebas Neue as a default, Trajan).
- Greeble HUD clutter that can't be read at trailer speed. GMUNK removed greeble so meaning read fast.
- Text on screen too short to read, or too long: more than 8 words or more than 2 lines on a card, or a sentence shown for under 0.25 s per word.
- Off-center focal points in sub-15-frame shots make viewers hunt and they disengage. Use center or eye-trace framing.
- Photosensitivity: more than 3 full-field flashes per second (WCAG 2.3.1 / Harding), especially saturated red. Keep strobes localized (25% of area or less) or low-contrast.
- Banding in dark fog and gradients at 8-bit or low bitrate. Add grain or dither, use CRF ≤ 16 with -tune grain, and render in half-float.
- Non-determinism (Math.random, performance.now, time-based noise effects) breaks frame-by-frame capture and motion-blur accumulation. Seed everything by frame index.
- Aliasing, shimmer and moire on thin lines and infinite grids. Supersample 1.5–2×, use fwidth anti-aliasing, and fade the grid with distance.
- Double tone mapping or a wrong output color space gives washed-out or crushed images. Use one tone-map step and sRGB output.
- Particle soup without form. Particles must resolve into a meaningful shape or flow along paths that tell the story.
- Scale without a reference. Huge objects with no tiny counterpart and no fog layers read small and cheap.
- Bouncy or overshoot easing on display type reads as a playful app UI, not a cinema title.
- Type colliding with letterbox bars, or outside the inner 90% safe area.
- AI visual clichés: glowing brains, blue humanoid robots, binary rain, circuit-board faces. The factory/line metaphor is more original and on-brief.
- Fabricated stats or benchmarks on cards destroy credibility. Use only verified claims, and keep claims short and concrete.
- Redrawing third-party or Anthropic logos inaccurately. Use a clean typographic lockup instead.

## CAMERA: CAMERA LANGUAGE FOR 3D/MOTION GRAPHICS (Three.js + HTML/SVG/GSAP, 30 fps deterministic capture). The camera is how the trailer gets its energy. Every move is a verb: push = tension/attention, pull = reveal/scale, crane = ascension/overview, track = process/progress, orbit = inspection/celebration, l

### Principles
- Every shot moves and every move means something. In a trailer a static frame is a deliberate event (the stillness before a hit), never a default. Pick one or two axes of motion per shot; moving in every axis at once reads as a screensaver.
- Match the camera grammar to the story arc. Problem act: handheld micro-noise, Dutch 6-12 deg, high angles on a tiny human, long lenses (85-200mm equivalent), cramped compositions, chaotic screen directions. Factory act: zero noise, level horizon, symmetry and one-point perspective, constant-velocity rails, wide lenses (14-24mm), deep focus, progress always left to right. Reveal: low-angle monument. Finale: god view. The audience should feel the camera itself become the factory: order replacing chaos.
- Energy comes from contrast across the cut, not from motion within the shot. Alternate scale (macro to god view), height (worm's-eye to top-down), direction, speed and brightness. Every montage cut must change at least 2 of {camera height, shot size, motion direction}. This is the 'top angle / bottom angle, bam bam bam' the brief asks for.
- Carry momentum through the cut. The outgoing shot accelerates into the cut (ease-in tail) and the incoming shot decelerates out of it (ease-out head) in the same screen direction, so velocity stays continuous and cuts feel like a single kinetic gesture.
- Stillness before impact. Before every tier-S hit, hold 12-24 frames with the camera locked or creeping linearly and the sound dropped out, then hit on the exact audio-transient frame. Contrast makes the hit.
- Scale is manufactured, not modeled. You need a known-size reference (a 1.8-unit human), exponential fog (atmospheric perspective), repetition receding to a vanishing point, a low camera, a wide lens, deep focus, and slow apparent motion. Big things move slowly: keep the structure's on-screen motion at or below 2-5% of its size per second. Shallow DOF on a wide shot creates the tilt-shift miniature effect and kills scale.
- Lens choice carries more emotion than position. Three.js default fov=50 (vertical) is about a 21mm lens on 16:9, which is why default WebGL scenes look like CG. Assign every shot a lens deliberately: 14-20mm for immersion and monuments, 35-50mm for neutral story, 85-300mm for compression, density and voyeur feel, and 75-95 deg vfov for speed.
- Radial moves (push/pull/dolly) beat lateral pans at 30 fps. Image motion near frame center stays small, so they strobe less. RED/ASC guidance: a pan slower than about 1 frame-width per 7 s is safe at 24 fps/180 deg. Pans should be either drift-slow, locked to a tracked subject, or whip-fast with heavy blur. Avoid the medium-speed judder zone.
- Abstract concepts need physics. Give them a body (thickness, bevels, rim light), a ground (dark reflective floor), atmosphere (fog, beams, dust), consistent units, cause and effect (pulses travel before nodes react, 3-6 frames per hop), and state change (shape or color transforms at gates). Disorder snapping into order is the core visual sentence of 'factory'.
- Always give the eye one hero to track across scale changes: one agent silhouette that is first alone, then 10, then 1,000, then a factory. Keep it centered through log zooms so the audience never loses the thread.
- Determinism: every camera parameter (position, target, roll, fov, focus, shake, FOV kick, flash, exposure) is a pure function of time t computed from a HITS list and shot table. No Math.random, no stateful lerps or springs, no requestAnimationFrame deltas. Otherwise seeking and sub-frame motion blur break.
- Sync to an integer-frame beat grid. At 30 fps use 120 BPM (beat = 15 f, bar = 60 f), or 90/100/112.5/150/180 BPM (20/18/16/12/10 f). Land the visual hit on the same frame as the audio transient or 1 frame early. ITU-R BT.1359 detectability is roughly audio 45 ms early to 125 ms late.
- Readability windows: while kinetic type is on screen, either camera angular velocity stays under about 3 deg/s or the type rides in world space with the scene. The eye cannot read and track a fast move at once.
- Restraint is currency. Use big shakes, dolly zooms, flashes and Dutch tilts for 3-5 key moments. If every cut shakes, nothing hits (GMUNK on Tron: Legacy UI: 'take out the greeble'; complexity 'sacrifices the quick read').

### Techniques
- **Lens map (focal length to vfov to emotion)**. *When:* Choosing the lens for every shot before blocking. Never leave fov at the default 50.
  *How:* three.js fov is VERTICAL, in degrees. With 16:9 and filmGauge 35 (camera.setFocalLength(mm) after setting aspect), the vertical fov is: 12mm=78.7, 14mm=70.2, 18mm=57.3, 21mm=50.2, 24mm=44.6, 28mm=38.7, 35mm=31.4, 50mm=22.3, 85mm=13.2, 100mm=11.2, 135mm=8.3, 200mm=5.6, 300mm=3.8. Assign by intent: monument/immersion 14-20mm placed CLOSE and LOW. Neutral story 35-50mm. Compression, density, swarms, queues and backlog walls 135-300mm from 5-10x the normal distance. Speed/POV fly-through 75-95 deg vfov. Above 100 deg vfov edges stretch, so avoid it. In a montage never put two consecutive shots in the same lens bracket.
  *Reference:* Dune (2021) and Blade Runner 2049 (2017) trailers alternate ultra-wide monument wides with long-lens compressed shots. Steal that alternation for the problem/solution montage.
- **Low-angle monument (worm's-eye)**. *When:* Power and arrival: the Opus 5.5 reveal, the factory as a cathedral. In the problem act, use it with converging verticals for menace (the backlog tower).
  *How:* Camera height 0.5-3% of subject height (subject 120 units tall: camera y = 1-3). Lens 14-20mm. Aim the lookAt at 60-70% of subject height (tilt up 10-25 deg). Symmetrical framing. Backlight/rim from behind the top edge so a light crest blooms over the silhouette. Move: creep push-in of 8-15% distance over 3-5 s, sine.inOut or linear, cut ON the hit. Roll stays 0 (level = authority; Dutch = threat). For calm architectural power, raise the frame with a lens shift (camera.setViewOffset with a y offset of 10-20% of height) instead of tilting, so verticals stay parallel. For looming menace, tilt so verticals converge. Put 50-200 dust motes 1-5 units from the lens, and set exponential fog so the subject's top fades 20-40%.
  *Reference:* 2001: A Space Odyssey: the monolith alignment shot (low, symmetrical, light cresting the top edge). Blade Runner 2049: K beneath the giant hologram. Steal the dead-center symmetry plus the light crest for the Opus title moment.
- **High angle and top-down god view**. *When:* A 30-60 deg high angle on the lone human gives vulnerability in the problem act. A 90 deg top-down gives the 'system / whole line at once' moment, the diagram beat and the finale.
  *How:* High angle: pitch -30 to -60 deg, 24-35mm, human at 2-5% of frame height. Top-down: pitch -90 deg. You MUST set camera.up=(0,0,-1) (or use pitch -89.5 deg), otherwise lookAt is degenerate and flips. Add a slow yaw rotation of 3-8 deg/s or a slow descent of 5-10% height per shot. At -90 deg the image reads as a map/schematic (use that deliberately); -65 to -80 deg keeps depth. Always deep focus. 135mm+ straight down flattens the factory into a circuit-board pattern. Perspective-to-blueprint morph: dolly-zoom fov 40 deg to 1 deg while distance grows about 40x (near/far scaled), and the factory becomes an orthographic diagram. Reverse it to bring the diagram back to life.
  *Reference:* Factorio/Satisfactory trailers (top-down conveyor sprawl). Dune top-down desert shots. Steal the slowly rotating top-down over moving lines.
- **Creep push-in (tension)**. *When:* Problem-statement holds, any type card, the 1-2 s before a hit.
  *How:* Distance -5 to -15% (or fov -5 to -10%) over 2-6 s, linear or sine.inOut (CSS cubic-bezier(0.37,0,0.63,1)). It must END on the hit frame; do not let it settle first. In 1 s montage shots push 8-12% so the motion registers. Dolly along the view axis gives low judder at 30 fps.
  *Reference:* The standard Nolan/Zimmer trailer hold before a BRAAM (Inception 2010, Interstellar 2014 trailers).
- **Crash / snap zoom**. *When:* Punctuation hits: 'BLOCKED', a number slamming in, a cut to an alarm.
  *How:* Anticipation: pull back 3% over 3-4 f (power2.out). Then fov x0.55 (or distance x0.4) over 4-6 f with power4.in / expo.in (cubic-bezier(0.7,0,0.84,0)), landing on the audio-transient frame. Tier-A impact: trauma 0.6, 1-frame exposure x2, shutter 270-360 deg. Settle with a damped spring (zeta 0.5, 6 Hz) over 6-10 f.
  *Reference:* Edgar Wright-style crash zooms; action-trailer 'stinger' cuts.
- **Pull-out reveal**. *When:* Scale reveals that start ON a hit: the factory, the fleet, the size of the backlog.
  *How:* Start at max velocity on the hit frame and use expo.out (cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)) over 2-4 s. Distance x3-10 with a slight crane up (+10-20% height) and fov widening +5 deg. If the distance ratio is above 5, interpolate in log space (next technique). The reveal should end 0.5 s before the next cut (a brief settle) or be cut while still drifting.
  *Reference:* Contact (1997) opening pull-out. Steal the 'never stop moving' feel.
- **Powers-of-Ten log zoom**. *When:* One agent to a fleet to a factory. A macro cursor to an entire delivery line. The cleanest single shot for 'scale changes everything'.
  *How:* d(u) = d0 * (d1/d0)^ease(u) with ease = power2.inOut (do NOT stack expo on top of log). Eames ran 10 s per decade outward and 2 s per decade inward; for a trailer use 0.6-1.2 s per decade, e.g., 3 decades (d 0.5 to 500) in 2.5-4 s. Every frame: near = d*0.01, far = d*1000. Swap level-of-detail per decade (hero agent mesh, then InstancedMesh, then shader point field, then baked texture), crossfading through fog. Keep lookAt fixed on the hero so the eye has an anchor the entire time.
  *Reference:* Powers of Ten (Eames, 1977): one power of ten per 10 s outward, 1 m overhead picnic start. Steal the top-down start and constant-perceived-speed log zoom, compressed about 10x.
- **Dolly zoom (Vertigo)**. *When:* Realization beats: 'capability to budget in ~5 months'; 'that was only the first wave'; the background factory swelling behind Opus.
  *How:* Lock frame height H = 2*d0*tan(fov0/2). Each frame: fov = 2*atan(H/(2*d)) in degrees, then camera.updateProjectionMatrix(). Distance x2-3 over 1.5-3 s, sine.inOut. Example: d 20 to 10 takes fov 50 to 86 deg (15 gives 63.7). Needs strong depth (background at 5-20x the subject distance) and parallel lines or repeated modules to read. Keep the subject dead center; never exceed about 100 deg vfov. Measure distance camera-to-subject, not camera-to-orbit-target.
  *Reference:* Jaws (1975) beach dolly zoom; Vertigo (1958). Use it exactly once or twice.
- **Orbit / arc and bullet time**. *When:* Inspection of a station, celebrating the assembled fleet (hero orbit), freezing a moment.
  *How:* Spherical coordinates around the target: eye = tgt + r*[cos(phi) sin(theta), sin(phi), cos(phi) cos(theta)]. Hero arcs 20-60 deg in 2-4 s sine.inOut; the 'assembly' orbit is 90-180 deg in 3-5 s; montage fragments run linear. Elevation 5-15 deg BELOW subject center reads heroic. Bullet time uses two clocks: worldClock speed 0.03-0.1x while the camera orbits 60-120 deg over 1.5 s at full speed. Avoid continuous 360 turntables (product-configurator look). Keep camera.up stable when crossing the top.
  *Reference:* The Avengers (2012) circular orbit around the assembled team; The Matrix (1999) bullet time. Steal the orbit at the moment the fleet locks into formation.
- **Crane up (ascension / context)**. *When:* Hierarchy (workers to leads to orchestrator), 'rising over the wall' reveals, the ending.
  *How:* Rise 0.5-3x subject height over 2.5-4 s, power2.inOut (cubic-bezier(0.65,0,0.35,1)). Either tilt down to hold the subject (a context reveal) or tilt up to follow a beam (ascension), leveling off at the top tier. Wall reveal: a foreground occluder (panel or wall) sinks below the bottom frame edge in the first 30% of the move, exposing the vista. Ending: crane up while tilting down until pitch is -90 deg, then hold the god view.
  *Reference:* Classic Western/epic crane-over-the-ridge reveals; Blade Runner 2049 spinner rises.
- **Tracking along the line (truck on a rail)**. *When:* Process and pipelines, the '5 months' road, the whole machine act.
  *How:* Camera on a rail parallel to the conveyor at a 3-8 unit offset, CONSTANT velocity (linear ease = machine precision), lens 85-135mm to compress the stations. Match speed with a work card, then overtake at 1.2-2x via power1.in. Foreground pillars spaced so one passes per beat (spacing = camera speed x 0.5 s at 120 BPM) for rhythmic wipes. Screen direction: progress is always left to right in the factory act; the problem act uses right-to-left or mixed directions.
  *Reference:* Kubrick/Wes Anderson lateral tracks; Apple product films' slow lateral drifts past detail. Steal the constant-velocity rail and perfect horizon.
- **Fly-through / POV ride**. *When:* Riding a work item through the pipeline, flying through UI layers or gates, a message traveling a graph edge.
  *How:* new THREE.CatmullRomCurve3(points, false, 'centripetal'); set curve.arcLengthDivisions = 2000; position = curve.getPointAt(u) (arc-length, so constant speed; getPoint(t) is NOT); target = curve.getPointAt(min(u+0.02,1)) for look-ahead; bank roll = k*curvature, clamped to 15 deg. vfov 75-95 deg. Near-field objects pass within 0.5-3 units every 8-15 frames (speed needs near references; an empty void reads as static). Place gates at arc lengths s(beat_i) so each pass lands on a beat, with flash + whoosh. Ramp speed 1 to 2.5x into the cut. Shutter 270 deg.
  *Reference:* Tron: Legacy (2010) grid and data-ring flights (GMUNK: packets go from disorder to concentric rings as the camera moves through them); the Star Wars trench run.
- **Dutch tilt and 'righting the camera'**. *When:* Problem act only: broken process, overload, unease.
  *How:* Roll 6-12 deg (subtle), 20 deg maximum; alternate the sign shot to shot. At the turn, on a tier-S hit, roll goes to 0 over 10-15 f with power3.out: the camera literally rights itself, a visual metaphor for order. Never use Dutch in the factory act. Combine low + Dutch for claustrophobic menace.
  *Reference:* Mission: Impossible Dutch + low angles (per NFI's camera-angle guide).
- **Rack focus**. *When:* Shifting attention: human to the first agent, a card to the factory behind it, type to world.
  *How:* BokehPass: animate uniforms.focus from dNear to dFar over 12-24 f with power2.inOut. Compute focus as depth ALONG the view direction: dot(target - camPos, camDir). Tune aperture to scene units (often very small, e.g., 0.002-0.03). Add a 1-2% push during the rack. For 2D/SVG layers, swap CSS blur 0 to 8 px between layers. Use the better webgl_postprocessing_dof2 shader for hero macro shots. Never use shallow DOF on wide scale shots.
  *Reference:* Apple product films: shallow-DOF macro passes then a focus reveal of the full device.
- **Parallax layers (2.5D) for type scenes**. *When:* Kinetic typography in HTML/SVG, and compositing type over 3D plates.
  *How:* 4-6 layers with parallax factors: bg 0.1, far 0.3, mid 0.6, type 1.0, foreground 1.6-2.0. Camera truck 3-8% of frame width per second: layer.x = -camX*factor. Push: layer.scale = 1 + push*factor. Blur the foreground 6-12 px and the background 2-4 px; type stays sharp. Use a foreground particle/bokeh layer crossing the type for depth. Rack focus = crossfade the blur values.
  *Reference:* Prologue/Elastic-style title sequences; any modern trailer title card with drifting dust in front.
- **Scale contrast (tiny human vs huge system)**. *When:* Problem act (human dwarfed by backlog), the turn (human before the factory), finale.
  *How:* Human 1.8 units; structure 100-500 units (ratio at least 1:50, ideally 1:150). Human at 2-5% of frame height on a lower-third intersection, backlit as a silhouette. FogExp2 density tuned so the structure's top loses 60-80% contrast. Repeated modules to the vanishing point. Slow camera. Deep focus. Low camera for awe, high for overwhelm.
  *Reference:* Blade Runner 2049 (K vs the giant statues in orange haze), Dune, Arrival (2016, tiny people under the shells).
- **Macro detail inserts**. *When:* Credibility and texture between wides: the blinking cursor, a card transforming at a gate, the etched Opus mark, a progress bar stuck at 97%.
  *How:* Make the camera tiny relative to the object (distance 0.05-0.3 object sizes), 50-100mm equivalent, shallow DOF, lateral drift of 1-3% object size per second. The object MUST have micro detail (etched glyphs, bevels, normal-mapped scanlines, specular sliding across edges); a macro of a smooth primitive looks empty. Add bokeh dust. 15-45 f in montage. A macro-to-god-view cut is the strongest size contrast available.
  *Reference:* Apple Silicon reveal films (CG die fly-overs, then pull-out to product); Oppenheimer trailer's abstract particle/energy inserts between story beats.
- **'Those aren't mountains' scale re-read**. *When:* THE turn: the coding-agent wave was the first wave; the factory is the next, much bigger one.
  *How:* Frame what reads as background terrain or sky (a crest fogged at 80%) behind the passing first-wave swell. Hold 1-2 s. Then tilt/crane up 25-40 deg over 2-3 s with power2.inOut, revealing that the 'background' is a moving structure 10-50x bigger. Lights on it ignite in a radial cascade. Tier-S braam on reveal completion, cut to black for 15 f, then cut to a reverse low angle.
  *Reference:* Interstellar (2014) Miller's planet: 'Those aren't mountains. They're waves.' Steal the misread-then-reframe reveal.
- **Whip pan / swish transition**. *When:* Joining high-energy montage shots and hiding scene changes.
  *How:* Outgoing: yaw accelerates from 0 to 40-90 deg/frame over 6-8 f (power3.in); cut at peak blur. Incoming: same direction, 6-8 f power3.out decel. Shutter 360 deg, 16-32 subframes, plus a cheap directional-blur post for the 2 peak frames. Always pair with a whoosh peaking on the cut frame.
  *Reference:* Guy Ritchie/Edgar Wright whip transitions; superhero trailer montage swishes.
- **Momentum-matched cut**. *When:* Every cut in a montage section.
  *How:* The outgoing shot's last 8-12 f use power2.in in direction D. The incoming shot's first 8-12 f use power2.out in the same screen direction D, with equal screen-space velocity at the cut (pixels/frame of the main subject). Alternative: a contrast cut where you deliberately reverse direction on a tier-S hit.
  *Reference:* Mad Max: Fury Road center-framed action editing (the eye stays put across fast cuts).
- **Angle ping-pong montage (top angle / bottom angle)**. *When:* Bam-bam sections (problem escalation; factory montage).
  *How:* Shot lengths 12-30 f, cut on beats (15 f at 120 BPM). Each next shot changes at least 2 of {height: worm/eye/high/top; size: macro/close/medium/wide/extreme; motion: L/R/in/out/rotate} and obeys the 30-degree rule (camera direction changes by 30 deg or more, or size changes by 2 steps). Template cycle: [worm/wide/push-in] then [top/extreme-wide/rotate] then [eye/macro/truck R] then [high/medium/crane] then [worm/close/orbit] then [top/wide/descend]. Never repeat a lens bracket or an angle back-to-back.
  *Reference:* Dune: Part Two and Top Gun: Maverick trailers' rapid alternation of low hero frames and high/aerial frames.
- **Speed ramp / time remap**. *When:* The 6-30 frames around tier-S hits; bullet-time moments.
  *How:* Two clocks: worldTime and camTime, each the integral of a speed curve, evaluated analytically per segment. Ramp: speed 1 to 0.15 over 6 f, hold 0.15 for 10-20 f, then snap to 1.5x with expo.in exactly on the hit, settling to 1x over 15 f. Scale the shutter with speed: at 0.15x use a small shutter (crisp detail); at 1.5-2x use 270-360 deg.
  *Reference:* 300 (2006) and Zack Snyder trailers' ramp-to-impact; The Matrix bullet time.
- **Impact kit (shake + FOV kick + flash), Eiserloh trauma model**. *When:* Every musical hit, scaled by tier. Big shakes on only 3-5 tier-S hits in the whole trailer.
  *How:* Closed form, no state. trauma(t) = min(1, sum over hits of amp*max(0, 1-(t-t_hit)/len)). shake = trauma^2 (squared, or cubed for punchier). Rotational shake for 3D (translation moves the horizon too much): yaw = 2.5 deg*shake*n1(t*f), pitch = 2.0 deg*shake*n2(t*f), roll = 3.5 deg*shake*n3(t*f), with seeded smooth noise. f must stay at or below 10-12 Hz unless sub-frame blur is on (30 fps Nyquist is 15 Hz). FOV kick: dFov(t) = -K*exp(-zeta*w*dt)*cos(wd*dt), K = 3-6 deg, f = 5 Hz, zeta = 0.4. Tiers: S (braam) amp 1.0, len 1.2 s, K 6 deg, 2-frame flash (exposure x4 decaying over 4 f), chromatic aberration 0.008 to 0 over 8 f, bloom x2 decaying over 10 f. A (hit) amp 0.6, len 0.6 s, K 3 deg, 1-frame flash x2. B (kick/tick) no shake; 2% push-in punch springing back over 6 f, or exposure +20% for 1 f.
  *Reference:* Squirrel Eiserloh, 'Math for Game Programmers: Juicing Your Cameras With Math' (GDC 2016): trauma 0-1, shake = trauma^2 or ^3, Perlin noise, rotational shake preferred in 3D.
- **Sub-frame accumulation motion blur + free AA**. *When:* All final renders. Mandatory for shakes, whips, fly-throughs and crash zooms; without it shake looks like a video game.
  *How:* For each output frame, render N subframes at t_k = frame/30 + ((k+0.5)/N - 0.5)*shutter/30 (shutter 0.5 = 180 deg default; 0.75-1.0 for whips/fly-throughs; 0.25 for crisp slow-mo). Re-evaluate the ENTIRE world (camera, particles, shaders) at t_k. Render each subframe into a HalfFloat target with NoToneMapping and add it with weight 1/N into an accumulation target. Then run bloom/grade/grain/tonemap/OutputPass once on the average (average in linear HDR, tone map after). Add Halton(2,3) subpixel jitter per subframe via camera.setViewOffset for free antialiasing of thin glowing lines. Adaptive N: estimate the largest screen motion per shutter (camera angular velocity / hfov x width, plus the fastest object) and set N = clamp(ceil(px/2), 4, 32).
  *Reference:* Blender EEVEE 'Accumulation Motion Blur' (time steps averaged; shutter expressed in frames) is the same model.
- **Foreground occlusion wipe (hidden cut)**. *When:* Faking 'one continuous take' between scenes, and rhythmic transitions in tracking shots.
  *How:* A dark foreground object (pillar, panel, swarm cloud, wave crest) passes within 0.3-1.0 units of the lens over 6-10 frames, covering 100% of the frame for at least 1 frame. Cut on the covered frame. The next shot starts with an occluder exiting in the same direction. Add a whoosh peaking on the covered frame.
  *Reference:* 1917 (2019) and Rope (1948) hidden cuts; Children of Men long takes.
- **Handheld vs motion-control grammar switch**. *When:* Making the story change visible in the camera itself: human chaos, then machine order.
  *How:* HUMAN (problem act): yaw/pitch noise 0.15-0.4 deg at 0.3-1.2 Hz, roll 0.2 deg, plus small 'operator correction' lags (target lerp delay of 4-8 f, computed analytically as a filtered path), Dutch 6-12 deg, slightly off-center compositions. MACHINE (factory act): zero noise, perfect constant-velocity or exactly eased moves, level horizon, symmetry and one-point perspective, moves that start and end exactly on beats. Switch on the tier-S hit at the turn: noise amplitude goes to 0 over 10 f together with roll to 0.
  *Reference:* ManvsMachine deliberately added handheld moves to CG for an analog feel (reMarkable Paper Pro) and constrained cameras to studio-camera limits for Toyota iQ. Same tool, opposite meaning.
- **Telephoto compression for swarms, queues and backlogs**. *When:* Making a crowd of tickets, PRs or agents feel overwhelming (problem) or massive and organized (solution).
  *How:* 135-300mm equivalent (vfov 8.3-3.8 deg), camera 5-10x farther than normal. Objects stack into a wall and depth collapses. Reduce fog density by the same distance factor so it does not white out. A slow push or truck of 1-2% per second. Add heat-shimmer or a slight vertical bob on the stacked objects so the wall feels alive.
  *Reference:* World War Z trailer zombie-pile telephoto shots; long-lens traffic/crowd shots in Koyaanisqatsi.
- **Abstraction vocabulary: AGENTS as physical bodies**. *When:* Any shot that has to show 'an agent'. Build this once and reuse it at every scale.
  *How:* One consistent silhouette: an emissive core (sphere r=0.1) inside a faceted shell (icosahedron detail 0, glass or edge-lit), a ribbon trail of the last 12-20 positions, and a fake 'work light' (an additive decal on the floor and the card it works on). State encoding: idle dims to 30%; working pulses at 2 Hz (or on the beat); done gives a 2-frame flash and a color shift. Scale: hero mesh at 1, InstancedMesh up to about 50k, GPU points beyond that. Escalate the count by about 10x on successive hits (1, 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000) so number becomes rhythm. Avoid glowing-brain and humanoid-robot clichés.
  *Reference:* Google DeepMind 'Visualising AI' (Unsplash/Pexels): small cubes for data, enclosing grids for training sets, inputs dropping into an abstract object. Abstract but tactile.
- **Abstraction vocabulary: PIPELINES, INTERFACES, HIERARCHIES, GRAPHS**. *When:* Turning 'software delivery line', 'fleet orchestration' and 'tool calls' into shootable sets.
  *How:* PIPELINE: TubeGeometry r=0.05 along a centripetal CatmullRom with a scrolling dash shader (fract(uv.x*30 - t*speed)). Stations are TorusGeometry gates; work cards (rounded boxes with canvas-texture text) change state at each gate: wireframe spec, then solid code, then edge-lit tested, then launched upward as a light beam. INTERFACES: glass panels (additive planes or MeshPhysicalMaterial transmission) stacked with 0.3-1.0 unit z-gaps and gently curved, with code/diff canvas textures; fly THROUGH them, each crossing giving a flash + whoosh. HIERARCHY: architecture: concentric tiers stacked like a ziggurat/cathedral, orchestrator at the apex, light beams descending to worker tiers; shoot with a crane up, or a worm's-eye from the bottom tier. GRAPH: precomputed (deterministic) 3D force layout; nodes are spheres with halo sprites; edges are light filaments carrying pulses that arrive 3-6 f before the target node blooms; ride an edge as the message. FACTORY HALL: an instanced modular bay repeated 40-200x into FogExp2, floor light strips converging to a one-point vanishing point. GROUND everything with a dark reflective floor (roughness 0.25-0.4) that catches the glows; this single choice makes abstract things feel physical.
  *Reference:* Tron: Legacy holographic UI (glowing blue restraint, disorder-to-order data rings); Oblivion screen graphics (function over decoration, palette readable on light and dark).
- **Swarm disorder to order (the assembly beat)**. *When:* The core 'factory' moment: chaotic agents snap into formation on a hit; also for the title (the swarm assembles the words).
  *How:* Each particle i has a curl-noise position N_i(t) and a formation target F_i (grid, ring, tier, or points sampled from rasterized text). p_i = mix(N_i(t), F_i, ease(clamp((t - t_hit - delay_i)/0.6, 0, 1))) with delay_i = 0.15 s * (distance from center / radius), a radial wave; ease power3.out. Lock-in lands on the downbeat with a tier-A impact. Shoot with telephoto compression or a slow 30 deg orbit, with a top-down cut immediately after to show the pattern.
  *Reference:* GMUNK's Tron: Legacy data visualizations: scattered packets gather into concentric rings of decoded data as the camera moves through them.

// 1. deterministic smooth noise
const n1=(x,s)=>(Math.sin(x*1.0+s*12.9)+0.5*Math.sin(x*2.37+s*78.2)+0.25*Math.sin(x*5.71+s*37.7))/1.75;
// 2. hits (built from the music cue sheet; same list drives audio)
const HITS=[{t:4.0,amp:0.5,len:0.6,K:3},{t:62.0,amp:1.0,len:1.2,K:6}, ...];
const trauma=t=>Math.min(1,HITS.reduce((a,h)=>{const d=t-h.t;return a+(d>=0&&d<h.len?h.amp*(1-d/h.len):0)},0));
const fovKick=t=>HITS.reduce((a,h)=>{const d=t-h.t;if(d<0)return a;const w=2*Math.PI*5,z=0.4,wd=w*Math.sqrt(1-z*z);return a-h.K*Math.exp(-z*w*d)*Math.cos(wd*d)},0);
// 3. camera evaluation
function camAt(t){ const {shot,u}=shotAt(t); const e=EASE[shot.ease](u);
 let eye=shot.path.getPointAt(e), tgt=shot.look.getPointAt(e); // CatmullRomCurve3(pts,false,'centripetal'), arcLengthDivisions=2000
 if(shot.log){const d=shot.d0*Math.pow(shot.d1/shot.d0,e); eye=tgt.clone().addScaledVector(shot.dir,d); cam.near=d*0.01; cam.far=d*1000;}
 let fov=shot.dolly? 2*Math.atan(shot.H/(2*eye.distanceTo(tgt)))*180/Math.PI : lerp(shot.fov0,shot.fov1,e);
 const k=trauma(t)**2, f=11; const hh=shot.handheld?1:0;
 const yaw=DEG*(2.5*k*n1(t*f,1)+0.3*hh*n1(t*0.7,4)), pitch=DEG*(2.0*k*n1(t*f,2)+0.25*hh*n1(t*0.9,5)), roll=DEG*(shot.dutch(u)+3.5*k*n1(t*f*0.8,3)+0.2*hh*n1(t*0.5,6));

 cam.position.copy(eye); cam.quaternion.copy(q); cam.fov=fov+fovKick(t); cam.updateProjectionMatrix(); }

### Pitfalls
- Leaving fov at three.js default 50 (about a 21mm lens) for everything gives the generic 'WebGL demo' look. Vary lenses per shot.
- Sine ease-in-out on every move, all the same length, gives a floaty screensaver/product-configurator feel. Use asymmetric eases (expo.out reveals, expo.in crash zooms), linear machine moves, holds, and moves that end ON the cut instead of settling.
- Continuous 360 turntable orbits and slow drifting cameras with no destination. Every move needs a start state, an end state and a reason.
- Medium-speed pans or trucks without enough motion blur judder at 30 fps (RED/ASC 7-second rule). Go drift-slow, track a subject, or whip.
- Shake without sub-frame motion blur looks like a video game. Shake noise above about 12 Hz without blur aliases into random jitter (Nyquist 15 Hz). Translational shake on wide shots wobbles the horizon; use rotational shake.
- Stateful shakes, springs, lerps or Math.random() in particle systems break deterministic capture, seeking and sub-frame accumulation. Every value must be f(t). Seed every random.
- lookAt straight down with up=(0,1,0) is degenerate and the camera flips or spins. Set camera.up=(0,0,-1) for top-down, and watch for flips when orbiting over the pole.
- CatmullRomCurve3.getPoint(t) is NOT constant speed; use getPointAt(u) with arcLengthDivisions of about 2000 for long paths. The 'catmullrom' type with high tension overshoots and loops; use 'centripetal'.
- Log zooms across orders of magnitude without updating near/far cause z-fighting and clipping. Update both every frame and swap LODs per decade.
- Shallow DOF on wide/scale shots triggers the tilt-shift miniature effect and makes the factory look like a toy. Shallow DOF is for macro only.
- BokehPass focus is a single plane in scene units, and its aperture value is scale-sensitive (usually tiny). Large apertures cause banding that cycles back into focus. Compute focus as depth along the view direction, not Euclidean distance.
- Tone mapping or sRGB conversion before averaging subframes gives wrong, dark motion trails. Accumulate in linear HalfFloat, then tone map. Running bloom per subframe multiplies cost; run it once after accumulation unless DOF requires per-subframe post.
- Dutch tilts outside the problem act read as amateurish, and too many cause nausea. Reserve the 'righting' moment for the turn.
- Over-spending impacts: shaking, flashing and dolly-zooming on every beat makes nothing land. Keep 3-5 tier-S moments, use tier B kicks elsewhere, and always put stillness before tier S.
- Two consecutive montage shots with the same height, size and lens read as a jump cut. Enforce the 30-degree rule and the change-2-of-3 rule.
- Fast camera motion while type is on screen means nobody can read it. Slow the camera below about 3 deg/s or anchor the type in world space.
- A million undifferentiated particles at a wide angle read as dust or noise. They need formation, a hero to track, telephoto compression, and a count escalation the viewer can feel.
- Fly-throughs of empty space feel static. Speed needs near-field references passing the lens every 8-15 frames.
- Blue-glow hologram overload and glowing-brain/robot clichés. Follow GMUNK's 'take out the greeble' restraint and a constrained palette, and give abstractions physical grounding (a reflective floor, fog, rim light).
- Field of view above about 100 deg vertical stretches edges grotesquely during dolly zooms or fly-throughs. Clamp it.
- Hero reveal shots longer than about 5 s kill trailer energy. Cut while the camera is still moving.

## SOUND: TRAILER MUSIC AND SOUND DESIGN, AND HOW TO SYNTHESIZE IT IN NUMPY. This covers the anatomy of a launch-trailer score (pulse, riser, braam, hit, whoosh, reverse swell, tick, heartbeat, glitch, drop, silence, sting), the tempo, harmony and loudness rules, and a recipe for each sound that needs no samp

### Principles
- THE SOUNDTRACK IS THE EDIT GRID. Lock the music first and cut picture to it. At 30 fps and 48 kHz, 1 frame is exactly 1600 samples. At 120 BPM one beat is exactly 15 frames, one bar is 2.000 s (60 frames), a 4-bar phrase is 8 s, and 2:24 is 72 bars. Every hit, card and flash goes on a frame computed from a beat index.
- THREE ACTS THAT ESCALATE IN WAVES. Trailer houses describe an intriguing sound-design intro (about 30 s in theatrical cuts), then a faster section as characters arrive, then a steady build with more strings to the end (Bulkeley, via Scripps; Trailer Park's Lava Hong). For a web cut, shrink the intro to about 20 s and put a 3-4 s hook in front of it.
- ACCELERATE BY SUBDIVISION, NOT BY TEMPO. Keep 120 BPM all the way through and change the note density: quarter-note ticks (2/s) in Act I, 8ths (4/s) in early Act II, 16ths (8/s) in Act III, 32nd rolls and glitch slices in the hit run. This is the 'bam-bam-bam' feeling, and it never breaks the frame grid.
- LOUDNESS IS CONTRAST. Impacts only hit hard against quieter material. A track that stays loud all the way through leaves the hits nowhere to land (IRPR). Even theatrical Leq(m) is a time average: 'if you need something loud, you need an equal amount of signals soft before it' (film-mixing.com). Plan the short-term loudness curve before writing notes.
- THE TENSION-RELEASE CYCLE IS THE ENGINE. A riser of 2-8 s cuts hard (never fades) into a gap of 0-30 frames of true silence, then a hit lands on the downbeat. Repeat about every 16-40 s, and make each cycle bigger: longer riser, longer gap, more layers on the hit.
- SAVE THE ENERGY. A build that is already busy has no room to rise. Keep hits sparse in the build and spend the big ones on the culmination (Evenant trailer-music course).
- SOUND DESIGN DOES THE WORK, BUT KEEP ONE MOTIF. Modern trailer cues lean more on sound design and SFX than on choirs, but asked what matters most, a Position Music A&R said 'Always melody.' Write one 3-5 note motif in D minor and repeat it as a tick, a pulse, a string line and finally the logo chord.
- STORY-CODED SOUNDS. Each recurring device should mean something, as the Interstellar ticks do (every 1.25 s = 1 Earth day) and as Nolan's pocket watch does in Dunkirk. Here, the tick is the 5-month clock from capability to budget, the data chirps are the agents, and the 16th-note pulse is the factory line.
- DON'T RESOLVE. Build to a huge climax but leave it unresolved so the audience wants more (Sound on Sound, library music part 7). The final braam is a D power chord with no third, with a bright add9 shimmer on top.
- TRANSLATE TO SMALL SPEAKERS. Laptops and phones barely reproduce anything below about 120-150 Hz. Every sub element needs harmonic stand-ins (saturation and a 100-220 Hz 'knock'), and the braam needs a 420-1400 Hz rasp, or it disappears on half the audience's devices.
- RATION THE BRAAM. It has been a cliché since about 2013 (IndieWire called it 'nearly omnipresent', and THR reported saturation by 2014). Use at most 6 in 2:24, at least 4 bars apart, each one marking a story turn, the way Zimmer described it: used to advance the story, not as a transition.
- DETERMINISM. Use fixed RNG seeds per sound and place samples exactly from frame numbers. Render the audio from the same timeline JSON as the Three.js/GSAP capture, so a re-render is bit-identical and stays in sync.

### Techniques
- **Frame-locked tempo map and subdivision ladder**. *When:* First step, before any sound is generated. Drives both the audio renderer and the visual timeline.
  *How:* Use BPM = 1800 / frames_per_beat. 120 BPM gives 15 f/beat (default). 150 BPM gives 12 f/beat with 16ths at exactly 3 f. 112.5 BPM gives 16 f/beat with 16ths at exactly 4 f. Pick 120 and stay there. Event sample index = frame*1600, with beat b at frame 15*b. 8ths fall on 7.5 frames, so alternate 7 and 8 for visual cuts but keep audio sample-exact. Ladder: Act I quarter ticks; Act II 8th pulse, moving to 16ths halfway; Act III 16th ostinato plus hits on beats 1 and 3; hit run hits on every beat, then 8ths, then a 1/16-1/32-1/64 glitch roll. Sync tolerance (ITU-R BT.1359 detectability): audio may lead by at most about 45 ms or lag by about 125 ms, so never let audio lead picture by more than 1 frame.
  *Reference:* Oppenheimer: Göransson's opening uses an incremental tempo increase without percussion to produce anxiety, and the atom cue has 21 tempo changes (The Conversation, TheWrap). Take the feeling of acceleration, but get it here by subdividing on a fixed grid.
- **BRAAM (low brass blast)**. *When:* At most 6 times: the hook, each act turn, the midpoint drop, the 'it fits' harmonic lift, the title. Never twice within 4 bars (8 s).
  *How:* Root D1 = 36.71 Hz (Ableton: record between C1 and C2 and stack octaves). Oscillators: 3 stacks of polyBLEP saws. Stack 1 at the root: 7 voices spread ±18 cents plus random 2 cents, gain 1.0. Stack 2 an octave up: 5 voices ±14 c, gain 0.55. Stack 3 at the 12th (3×): 3 voices ±10 c, gain 0.25. Each voice gets a slow drift of 0.15% at 0.1-0.4 Hz. Use different seeds for L and R. Pitch envelope: scoop -0.6 st → 0 with tau 40 ms, then a quadratic droop of -2 st across the second half (Blade Runner 2049 style fall). Growl: AM at 31 Hz, depth 0.22. Pre-filter saturation tanh(3.5*(x+0.1)). Low-pass filter (RBJ, Q 1.1, coefficients updated every 32 samples): 150 → 2600 Hz exponentially in 80 ms (the 'blat'), then decaying to 750 Hz with tau 0.9 s. Add the formant: bandpass 420-1400 Hz of the filtered signal, tanh drive 2.5, mixed at 0.9. Sub: sine at the same pitch envelope, gain 0.35, tanh 1.6 to add a 3rd harmonic. Breath: pink noise bandpassed 500-1800 Hz, tau 0.25 s, 0.08. Amp: attack 45 ms (curve ^1.5), hold, release over the last 45% (^1.3). Reverb RT60 5 s, wet -6 dB, dark 1.2 kHz, HPF on the return at 150 Hz. Final tanh 1.3, peak -1 dBFS. Duration 4 s (2 bars) plus a 5 s tail. For the final title, use a power chord: render at D1 and A1 (55 Hz, -4 dB) and sum them.
  *Reference:* Inception trailer: the famous BRAAM comes at about 0:24 (Rareform). Vulture reported the score version as bassoon, French horn, trombone and tuba in unison plus timpani. Take a single note with apocalyptic weight, used as punctuation at story turns. Blade Runner 2049 trailers (sound design by Bulkeley): take the falling, detuned analog pitch droop.
- **Trailer HIT / impact (5 layers plus tail)**. *When:* On bar downbeats in Act II (every 60 f), beats 1 and 3 in Act III (every 30 f), every beat in the hit run (every 15 f), and under every braam.
  *How:* Layers, all starting at sample 0. (1) Transient: white noise HPF 2.5 kHz × exp(-t/4 ms), gain 0.9. (2) Body: sine sweeping 170 → 48 Hz (f = 48 + 122·e^(-t/35ms)) × exp(-t/0.22 s), tanh drive 3. (3) Sub boom: sine 58 → 28 Hz (tau 0.45 s), 3 ms attack, decay tau 0.9 s, kept DRY and MONO. (4) Crack: noise bandpassed 900-4500 Hz × exp(-t/70 ms), tanh 4, gain 0.5. (5) Metal: 5 sines at 310 Hz × [1, 2.32, 4.25, 6.63, 9.38] with decays [1.3, 0.9, 0.6, 0.45, 0.3] s and 1/k gains, sum × 0.25. (6) Knock for phones: noise bandpassed 100-220 Hz × exp(-t/50 ms), 0.8. Reverb only on layers 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6: RT60 4.5 s, wet -3 dB, HPF return 150 Hz. Total length 4.5 s. Variations: shift metal_f between 250 and 420 Hz and body start between 140 and 200 Hz per hit so a run doesn't sound machine-gunned. Ableton's guide does the same thing (kick or taiko base, metallic top, separate EQ per layer, long reverb tail).
  *Reference:* Ableton's 'High-Impact Sounds' tutorial uses five layers: distorted kick, high percussion, processed cymbal, sub, atmosphere. Violet Recording splits a hit into attack, body and low end. Oppenheimer trailer: big hits alternating with silence.
- **Sub drop under title cards**. *When:* Under the frame a card or title lands, under each braam, and at the end of the hit run.
  *How:* Sine with f(t) = 26 + 54·e^(-t/(dur/3.5)), so it falls 80 → 26 Hz over 2.5 s. 4 ms attack, decay tau dur/2.2. tanh drive 1.8 so the harmonics read on laptops. Place it at -4 to -6 dB relative to the hit. Mono and dry. Only one sub-heavy event may sound at a time; let the previous sub tail be cut by the gap.
  *Reference:* IRPR: 'felt rather than heard, which is why it works under a title card.' A sub drop on the frame a title lands does the job of a full score without committing the sequence to music.
- **Noise riser with tempo-doubling gate**. *When:* Into every act turn: 1 bar (2 s) for small turns, 2 bars (4 s) for act turns, 3-4 bars (6-8 s) for the climax.
  *How:* Pink noise (FFT 1/sqrt(f) shaping), separate seeds for L and R. Time-varying bandpass with center fc(p) = 250·(9000/250)^(p^1.6), where p = t/dur, and Q rising 0.9 → 3.9. Stereo width M + p^1.5·(S): it starts narrow and blooms wide. Gain -36 dB → 0 dB along p^2.2. Rhythmic gate locked to tempo: quarter notes for p < 0.5, 8ths to 0.75, 16ths to 0.9, then 32nds. Gate shape clip(1 - phase/0.7)^0.6, depth 65%. End it with a hard cut (no fade) 1 frame to 1/8 note (7 f) before the hit, using place_end(). Rhythmic risers 'accelerate a repeating gate so the increasing pulse rate signals the approaching climax' (IRPR glossary). Short 1-2 s risers suit fast social cuts; trailer builds run 4-8 s.
  *Reference:* Avengers: Age of Ultron teaser: distorted risers from Twelve Titans' 'Artifice' (A Sound Effect). Take the distortion: run the riser through tanh(2) in its last 25%.
- **Shepard-Risset glissando (the endless rise)**. *When:* As a tension bed under Act I (slow) and as the pitch layer of the climax riser (fast). This is the 'keep narrating the problem' device: it rises the whole time and never arrives.
  *How:* 9 octave-spaced components from base 27.5 Hz. pos_k(t) = (k + rate·t) mod 9, f_k = 27.5·2^pos_k, amplitude a_k = 0.5 - 0.5·cos(2π·pos_k/9), which is zero at both ends of the range so the wrap is inaudible. Integrate phase with cumsum so frequency changes never click. Each component is a sine plus harmonics 2 and 3 at 1/h^1.5, and you MUST mask any partial above 0.45·SR (the prototype aliased audibly before that mask was added). Rate: 0.06-0.12 oct/s as a bed (one octave every 8-16 s, the Dunkirk feel), 0.25-0.5 oct/s for a final 4-8 s riser. 1 s fade-in, reverb RT60 3 s at -10 dB. Mix the bed at -24 to -30 dB under the ticks.
  *Reference:* Dunkirk: Nolan describes the Shepard tone as a 'corkscrew', always going up but never leaving its range, and built the screenplay as its structural equivalent. The Dark Knight: the Batpod's sound is reportedly built on a Shepard tone (Richard King). Take the effect of perpetual escalation under the problem section.
- **Risset rhythm (a clock that accelerates forever)**. *When:* Under the '5 months from capability to budget' countdown, so time itself seems to speed up.
  *How:* 3 layers of the same tick at tempo bpm0·2^((k + rate·t) mod 3), with bpm0 = 60 and rate = 1/6 octave of tempo per second. Get each layer's tick times by integrating tempo (beat phase = cumsum(bpm/60/SR), fire on each integer crossing). Weight each tick by 0.5 - 0.5·cos(2π·pos/3). The result keeps speeding up without the rate ever running away. Mix at -20 dB rising to -12 dB across the countdown. For a version locked to the grid, crossfade a quarter-note tick stream into an 8th stream into a 16th stream over 2 bars each.
  *Reference:* Dunkirk: Nolan's own pocket-watch recording turned into a synth pulse, a 'double-time ticking... balancing act of how much is too much'. A separate tempo illusion (associated with Risset) is noted on jaapsch.net. The first Dunkirk teaser was built on that recording.
- **String swell / bowed-tremolo crescendo**. *When:* Together with the noise riser into Act turns, and as the 'emotional' layer when the solution arrives.
  *How:* Chord Dm (D3 A3 D4 F4 A4, MIDI 50 57 62 65 69), 6 polyBLEP saws per note at random ±14 c, each with vibrato at 4.8-6.2 Hz. Vibrato depth grows 0 → 0.15 st with progress. Random pan within ±0.8. Low-pass 350 → 7000 Hz along p^1.3, Q 0.8. Bowed tremolo: amp × (1 - 0.45·p²·(0.5 + 0.5·sin(2π·13 Hz·t))). Gain -40 → 0 dB along p^1.8. Hard cut at the hit. A single sustained note (D4 alone) with the same envelope gives the Dark Knight 'Joker' screw-tightening variant.
  *Reference:* The Dark Knight (2008) trailer: one rising string note as unbearable tension. Oppenheimer: violin clusters 'in an escalating crescendo of quivering energy' (Alison Cole).
- **Whoosh / swish (Doppler pass)**. *When:* On every hard cut in Acts II and III, on camera fly-throughs, and on typography slamming in. Put the PEAK on the cut frame, not the onset.
  *How:* Duration 0.3 s (9 f) for beat cuts, 0.8 s for 2-beat cuts, 1.2-2 s for 3D camera moves, with the peak at 62% of the duration. Band center rises 250 → 3800 Hz along (p/peak)^1.5, then falls to 375 Hz along ^0.7 (the Doppler dive). Layers: pink noise bandpass Q 2.2 (the air); white noise bandpass Q 12 at 1.6×fc, gain 0.25 (the whistle); pink low-passed at 350 Hz and high-passed at 60 Hz, gain 0.25 (the body; at 0.6 it swamped everything below 120 Hz). Envelope (p/peak)^3 up, then exp(-(t - t_peak)/0.11 s). Pan sweeps -0.9 → +0.9 with equal power, plus an interaural delay of up to 0.5 ms on the far ear. Place it at t_cut - 0.62·dur. Vary the seed per cut.
  *Reference:* film-mixing.com: a reverse drop whoosh from the title card into the opening. Farnell's Designing Sound treats this kind of effect as a procedural process (filtered noise plus a time-varying delay for Doppler, per JOS/CCRMA).
- **Reverse swell / suckback**. *When:* The last 0.5-2 s before any hit, braam or stop-down. Also the 'inhale' before the logo.
  *How:* Render the first 0.5 s of the target sound (usually the hit) through a reverb at RT60 3.5 s, 100% wet. Reverse it, keep the last 'length' seconds (1 beat = 0.5 s, or 1 bar = 2 s), multiply by a ramp^2, and use place_end() so it ends exactly on the hit sample. For a hard stop (the picture changes on the stop), end it on the cut and follow with 4-8 frames of silence.
  *Reference:* IRPR: 'growing backwards from silence into a hard stop. Lands the cut for you, so the picture can change on the stop.'
- **Pulse ostinato (the factory line)**. *When:* From Act II onward: 8ths first, 16ths from the middle of Act II, full 16ths with accents in Act III.
  *How:* Per note: two polyBLEP saws at ±7 cents plus a square one octave below (gain 0.6). Low-pass Q 1.4 with a per-note envelope 260 + 2600·e^(-t/50ms) Hz on downbeats and +1500 Hz on others. Amp: 2 ms attack, decay tau 90 ms. tanh 2. Downbeat accent +3 dB. Root D2 (MIDI 38) as a pedal. Bar offsets [0, -4, -2, -5] give Dm, Bb, C and A over the pedal. Patterns: 'x..x..x.x..x..x.' (3+3+2 grouping, the classic hybrid drive) and later 'x.xx.xx.x.xx.xx.'. Sidechain to the hit bus: duck 4-6 dB, attack 1-5 ms, release 250-400 ms. For a 'power-up', run tape_start over the first 0.6 s at the midpoint drop.
  *Reference:* Tron: Legacy trailers and score (Daft Punk): analog arpeggio pulse plus orchestra as the sound of 'the machine'. A Sound Effect cites the 'pulsating atmospheres' of the Titanfall trailer.
- **Ticking clock**. *When:* Act I bed (quarter notes), the countdown (Risset version), and the button after the title, where the tick alone returns.
  *How:* 80 ms sound. Click: white noise bandpassed 0.8-1.25×f0 × exp(-t/1.5 ms). Ring: sin(f0) + 0.5·sin(2.7·f0) × exp(-t/12 ms) × 0.3. Tick f0 = 3400 Hz, tock f0 = 2600 Hz, alternating. Keep it dry or in a 0.8 s room. Level -26 rising to -14 dB. Ticks sit in the 2-5 kHz presence band and cut through anything, so keep them quiet. Trailer sound blogs note the tick went out of fashion from overuse and came back as a staple, so make it mean something.
  *Reference:* Interstellar's 'Mountains': ticks every 1.25 s, each one an Earth day (NoFilmSchool). Dunkirk: the watch. Oppenheimer: a player tapping a small metal cup, plus Geiger-counter clicks (TheWrap).
- **Heartbeat**. *When:* Act I, under the human cost of the problem (teams waiting, queues). It drops out at the first braam.
  *How:* Lub: sine sweeping 60 → 40 Hz (tau 30 ms) × exp(-t/80 ms), tanh 2. Dub: 73 → 45 Hz (tau 25 ms) × exp(-t/60 ms), -4 dB, 220 ms after lub. Low-pass 220 Hz. Lock it to the grid at 60 BPM (every 2 beats) and rise to 120 (every beat) as stakes climb. Give it harmonic saturation, otherwise phones hear nothing.
  *Reference:* A standard thriller-trailer device. Pair it with the tick so 'time' and 'pressure' are two separate sounds.
- **Piano 'ping' (single reverberant note)**. *When:* Under each problem card in Act I, and in every stop-down. Also layered into the final sting.
  *How:* D6 (1175 Hz) or D5. Partials f_k = k·f0·sqrt(1 + 0.0004·k²) (string inharmonicity) for k up to 13, each decaying with tau 2.2/k^0.8 s at gain 1/k^1.1. Hammer: noise bandpassed 1.5-6 kHz × exp(-t/6 ms) × 0.3. Reverb RT60 6.5 s, predelay 30 ms, wet -2 dB. Level -10 to -12 dB. It sustains strongly around 1.2 kHz, so only one ping every 2 bars or the tails smear (seen in the test spectrogram).
  *Reference:* Rareform: the reverberant solo piano 'ping' went from game-changer to ubiquitous. A Sound Effect: reverb-heavy piano intros in Age of Ultron and The Force Awakens.
- **Agent 'data chirps' (FM telemetry)**. *When:* Whenever the visuals show agents or fleets working: code streaming, PRs merging, pipelines lighting up. It is this trailer's signature 'tech' texture.
  *How:* 8-16 blips on a 32nd grid (62.5 ms). Each blip lasts 20-60 ms. Carrier is D minor pentatonic in MIDI 74-96. FM ratio is one of {1.5, 2, 3.5}, index 2-6 decaying with tau 15 ms. Amp: 1 ms attack, tau 20 ms. Random pan within ±0.6. Level -14 to -18 dB. To show a fleet getting bigger, double the density (2 or 3 overlapping streams with different seeds).
  *Reference:* Prometheus teaser: an alarm used as a motif (Audiomachine's 'Judge and Jury'). Take the idea of a functional, machine-originated sound used as music.
- **Glitch stutter, buffer-repeat roll, bitcrush, tape stop and start**. *When:* On number changes in the 5-month countdown, on any 'system' visual glitch (2-4 frames), at the end of the hit run, and as a power-down before a stop-down.
  *How:* Buffer repeat: loop a slice with 2 ms fades. Roll slice lengths 1/16 (125 ms) → 1/32 (62.5 ms) → 1/64 (31.25 ms, about 1 frame). Repeating a 31.25 ms slice becomes a 32 Hz tone and 1/128 becomes 64 Hz, which is the 'stutter turns into pitch' rise. Bitcrush: quantize to 6 bits, sample-and-hold every 10 samples (4.8 kHz effective rate). Tape stop: playback rate 1 → 0 along linspace^1.6 over 0.3-0.6 s, done by np.interp on cumsum(rate). Tape start (power-up) is the same ramp from 0 → 1. Match the visual glitch exactly: a 2-frame visual glitch gets a 2-frame (66.7 ms) buffer repeat.
  *Reference:* A Sound Effect cites 'distortion booms and stutter downers' in the Elysium trailer. EDM beat-repeat practice.
- **The drop with an air pocket**. *When:* Every act turn. The bigger the moment, the longer the silence.
  *How:* Sequence: riser and string swell end with a hard cut 1/8 note (7-8 f) before the downbeat. Then hard-gate the whole music and SFX bus, including reverb tails, to digital zero (5 ms fade). Gap lengths: 8 f (0.27 s) at act turns, 15 f (0.5 s) at the midpoint, 30 f (1.0 s) before the title. Then braam + impact + sub drop on frame 0 of the next bar. Variant (fake-out): after the gap, play only a ping and a card for 1 bar, and slam on the next bar. That delays the payoff once, at most once per trailer.
  *Reference:* Violet Recording: 'cut to near-silence right before the final hit... dropping everything out for a beat makes the next impact feel enormous.' Oppenheimer: silence as 'an expression of dread'.
- **Stop-down for a text card (no-VO equivalent of a dialogue line)**. *When:* Two or three times: the key problem line, the 'that was the coding agent' line, the 'why Opus 5.5' line.
  *How:* On a bar line, cut music to zero (or to a -40 dB drone) for 1 bar (2 s = 60 f). The card lands on frame 0 of that bar together with a ping (-10 dB) and an optional single tick. A reverse swell (1 beat) re-enters on the next downbeat. The silence gives a sentence about 2 s of reading time with the viewer's full attention.
  *Reference:* Trailer-editing convention: music stops down for a line. film-mixing.com: dropping elements under dialogue 'our mind... will fill the gaps.'
- **Hit run ('bam-bam-bam') into the climax**. *When:* The 2 bars before the air pocket and title, once per trailer.
  *How:* Bar 1: an impact on each of the 4 beats (every 15 f) at -4 dB, alternating metal_f 310/370 Hz and body start 170/190 Hz, each with a 2-frame white flash and a hard cut. Bar 2, beats 1-2: impacts on 8ths (frames alternate 7/8), -2 dB. Bar 2, beats 3-4: glitch roll 1/16 → 1/32 → 1/64 on the pulse plus a noise riser at full level, with a Shepard layer at 0.5 oct/s underneath. Last 1/8: tape stop. Then the 30 f air pocket. Each hit triggers camera shake with amplitude decaying over tau of about 6 frames.
  *Reference:* Evenant: put a big impact at the start of each (or every second) bar, put whooshes, taiko rolls, cymbal rolls or a short rise in front of the main hits, and save the energy for the culmination.
- **Final sting, button and logo tail**. *When:* Title reveal, then a short button, then the end card.
  *How:* Sting = final braam (D1 and A1 power chord, no third) + biggest impact (0 dB) + sub drop (-4 dB) + logo_shimmer: a D major add9 bell (MIDI 62 69 74 78 81, partials 1/2/3.01 with taus 2.5/1.2/0.6 s, an octave-up bloom fading in over 1.5 s, RT60 7 s). Let it decay for 5-6 s. Button, 8 s after the title: the tick returns alone at -26 dB, the last card lands with a micro-sting (data chirp burst + a small hit at -12 dB + ping). Logo tail: the last 5 s decay below -60 dBFS by the final frame. Leave the harmony unresolved, or 'resolve' only to the open major add9, never a cadence.
  *Reference:* FilmEditingPro: the button is one last joke, action beat or line after the main title, before the end. IRPR: the logo tail 'carries the last five seconds after everything else has stopped'.
- **Harmony for 'epic tech'**. *When:* Writing the pulse, string and braam pitches. One key center the whole way through.
  *How:* D minor (Aeolian) over a D pedal. Braam root D1 = 36.71 Hz, pulse D2 = 73.42 Hz, ping D6 = 1175 Hz. Act I (problem): open fifth D-A drone plus a Phrygian neighbor Eb against D (a minor second reads as threat). Act II (story): i - bVI - bVII - i over the pedal (Dm, Bb/D, C/D, Dm), 2 bars per chord. Midpoint and Act III: i - bVI - bIII - bVII (Dm Bb F C), then at the 'why Opus 5.5' turn a chromatic lift bVI → I (Bb major → D major, Picardy-style), which marks the solution arriving. Final: D5 power chord braam with add9 shimmer. For a fast synth-melody motif, use D-F-A-G-F on 16ths. E minor (E1 = 41.2 Hz) also works and survives small speakers a little better than C (32.7 Hz).
  *Reference:* Sound on Sound / trailer composers: harmonically spare, densely produced; 'much more time working on the sound than on the composition' (Nicolai Patricio).
- **Mix hierarchy, ducking and frequency slots**. *When:* Throughout. Build three buses: MUSIC (pulse, strings, pings, ticks, shepard), SFX (whooshes, risers, chirps, glitches), HITS (impacts, braams, sub drops).
  *How:* The HITS bus is never ducked. It ducks MUSIC and SFX by 4-6 dB (attack 1-5 ms, release 250-400 ms) and is preceded by a pre-hit dip of -6 to -12 dB over the last 1/8 note. Frequency slots: 25-60 Hz (sub drops, braam fundamental, one at a time, mono, dry); 60-200 Hz (hit body, pulse); 200-500 Hz (keep clean: cut 3 dB at 300 Hz on music under hits); 420-2000 Hz (braam rasp, strings, pings); 2-5 kHz (ticks and transient clicks, used sparingly); 8-16 kHz (riser tops, whoosh air). Reverb: ticks 0-0.8 s, hits 3-5 s, braams 4-6 s, pings 6-8 s, logo 7 s, all returns high-passed at 150 Hz with 20-30 ms predelay. Don't layer too many sounds for one aspect, and avoid excess 2-8 kHz, which spikes loudness and fatigue (film-mixing.com).
  *Reference:* A Sound Effect (Jan Hoeglund, Audio Imperia): 'things can always be made louder at the end'; master-bus compression or multiband distortion 'within reason'.
- **Web master chain (tested)**. *When:* Final render of the full mix.
  *How:* Chain: HPF 28 Hz, 24 dB/oct (removes inaudible energy that eats headroom). Then +8 dB into a unity-small-signal soft clipper y = tanh(1.2x)/1.2. Then a look-ahead limiter: 5 ms lookahead, 150 ms release, ceiling -1.2 dBFS, with a TRUE-PEAK detector (4× resample_poly). Measured on the 40 s test: -14.3 LUFS-I, -1.2 dBTP, LRA 9.9 LU, short-term range -18 (Act I) to -7.9 (climax). Gain reduction was about 1 dB on music sections, 4.6 dB on the groove and 8 dB on the biggest drops (most of it from the clipper). If that is too crushed, accept -15 or -16 LUFS. Measure with: ffmpeg -i mix.wav -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -. Targets: -14 LUFS-I ±1 (common web rule of thumb; an alternative pro consensus is -16 LUFS / -2 dBTP / LRA ≤ 12), TP ≤ -1 dBTP, LRA 8-12 LU. Theatrical trailers are a different standard: 85 Leq(m) under TASA.
  *Reference:* In the test, a plain sample-peak limiter at -1.5 dBFS still measured 0.0 dBTP. Oversampled detection fixed it. Platforms normalize loud masters down, so mastering at -9 LUFS only buys squashed hits.
- **Small-speaker and mono translation check**. *When:* After every mix pass.
  *How:* (1) Fold to mono, (L+R)/2. Energy loss should be between 0 and -3 dB per element; anything below -3 dB means phase cancellation. Measured: braam -1.5, strings -0.5, riser -2.7, whoosh -2.6, full mix -0.8 dB. (2) Phone simulation: HPF 300 Hz plus LPF 8 kHz on the mix. Every hit, braam and sub drop must still be clearly audible, which is why each has a 100-220 Hz knock, saturation harmonics and a 420-1400 Hz rasp. (3) Keep sub and braam fundamental centered; only noise, strings and whooshes get wide.
  *Reference:* The prototype braam first had 92% of its energy below 120 Hz and would have vanished on laptops. Adding the formant layer and cutting the sub to 0.35 moved the centroid from 71 to 202 Hz.

=== C. NUMERIC RULES ===
Risers: 1 bar (minor turn) / 2 bars (act turn) / 3-4 bars (climax). End with a hard cut 1-8 f before the hit. Never fade out.
Air pockets: 8 f / 15 f / 30 f (turn / midpoint / title). They are true digital zero, not 'quiet'.
Whoosh: peak = cut frame; dur 9 f (beat cuts), 24 f (2-beat cuts), 36-60 f (camera moves).
Hits: no two big hits closer than 1 beat outside the hit run. Every big hit has at least 2 bars of lower energy before it.
Braams: ≤6 total, ≥4 bars apart, each on a story turn.
Ducking: HITS bus ducks MUSIC/SFX 4-6 dB, attack 1-5 ms, release 250-400 ms; pre-hit dip -6 to -12 dB over 1/8 note.
Section LUFS-S: hook -11 | Act I -24→-18 | Act II -17→-13 | stop-down -30 | Act III -13→-9 | hit run -8 | title momentary max -7 | tail → silence.
Master: I -14 LUFS (±1), TP ≤ -1.0 dBTP, LRA 8-12 LU. Biggest hits ≤ 6-8 dB GR, music ≤ 2 dB.

=== D. NUMPY CORE (excerpt of the tested trailer_sfx.py; full file in the scratchpad path above) ===
import numpy as np; from scipy import signal as S
SR=48000
tt=lambda d: np.arange(int(round(d*SR)))/SR
db=lambda x: 10**(x/20)
def polyblep_saw(freq, ph0=0.0):              # band-limited saw, freq = per-sample array
    dt=np.asarray(freq)/SR; ph=(ph0+np.cumsum(dt))%1.0; y=2*ph-1
    m=ph<dt; t=ph[m]/dt[m]; y[m]-=t+t-t*t-1
    m=ph>1-dt; t=(ph[m]-1)/dt[m]; y[m]-=t*t+t+t+1
    return y
sine=lambda f, p0=0.0: np.sin(p0+2*np.pi*np.cumsum(np.asarray(f))/SR)   # integrate phase so pitch envelopes never click
def biquad(kind,f,q):                          # RBJ cookbook
    w=2*np.pi*np.clip(f,10,SR*.45)/SR; c,s=np.cos(w),np.sin(w); a=s/(2*q)
    b={'low':[(1-c)/2,1-c,(1-c)/2],'high':[(1+c)/2,-(1+c),(1+c)/2],'band':[a,0,-a]}[kind]
    A=[1+a,-2*c,1-a]; return np.array(b)/A[0], np.array(A)/A[0]
def tv_filter(x,fc,kind='low',q=.707,block=32):  # time-varying filter: new coefs every 32 samples, state carried
    n=len(x); fc=np.broadcast_to(fc,(n,)); q=np.broadcast_to(q,(n,)); y=np.empty(n); zi=np.zeros(2)
    for i in range(0,n,block):
        b,a=biquad(kind,fc[i],q[i]); y[i:i+block],zi=S.lfilter(b,a,x[i:i+block],zi=zi)
    return y
sat=lambda x,d=3.,bias=0.: (np.tanh(d*(x+bias))-np.tanh(d*bias))/np.tanh(d)
def reverb_ir(rt60=4., predelay=.02, bright=9000, dark=1800, hpf=150, seed=3):
    r=np.random.default_rng(seed); n=int(rt60*1.1*SR); t=np.arange(n)/SR
    raw=r.standard_normal((2,n))*np.exp(-6.91*t/rt60)          # -60 dB at rt60
    lp=lambda f: S.sosfilt(S.butter(2,f,'lowpass',fs=SR,output='sos'),raw,axis=-1)
    w=np.clip(t/(.6*rt60),0,1); ir=lp(bright)*(1-w)+lp(dark)*w   # tail darkens over time
    ir=S.sosfilt(S.butter(2,hpf,'highpass',fs=SR,output='sos'),ir,axis=-1)  # no sub in tails
    ir=np.pad(ir,((0,0),(int(predelay*SR),0))); return ir/np.sqrt((ir**2).sum(1,keepdims=True))
# convolve each channel with S.fftconvolve; mix wet at -2…-8 dB (0.5x scale in the lib)

# SHEPARD-RISSET (rate in octaves/s; mask partials near Nyquist!)
def shepard(dur=16, base=27.5, octs=9, rate=.125, H=3):
    t=tt(dur); y=0
    for k in range(octs):
        pos=(k+rate*t)%octs; f=base*2**pos; a=.5-.5*np.cos(2*np.pi*pos/octs); p0=np.random.rand()*6.28
        for h in range(1,H+1): y=y+a*(f*h<.45*SR)*sine(f*h,p0)/h**1.5
    return y*np.clip(t,0,1)

# NOISE RISER (pink noise, tempo-doubling gate)
def noise_riser(dur=8, f0=250, f1=9000, bpm=120):
    t=tt(dur); p=t/dur; fc=f0*(f1/f0)**(p**1.6); x=tv_filter(pink(len(t)),fc,'band',.9+3*p)
    div=np.select([p<.5,p<.75,p<.9],[1,2,4],8); ph=np.cumsum(div/(60/bpm)/SR)%1
    return x*db(-36+36*p**2.2)*(.35+.65*np.clip(1-ph/.7,0,1)**.6)

# WHOOSH (place at t_cut - peak*dur)
def whoosh(dur=.8, peak=.62, f0=250, f1=3800):
    t=tt(dur); p=t/dur
    fc=np.where(p<peak, f0*(f1/f0)**((p/peak)**1.5), f1*((1.5*f0)/f1)**(np.clip((p-peak)/(1-peak),0,1)**.7))
    x=tv_filter(pink(len(t)),fc,'band',2.2)+.25*tv_filter(np.random.randn(len(t)),1.6*fc,'band',12)
    env=np.where(p<peak,(p/peak)**3,np.exp(-(t-peak*dur)/.11)); return x*env   # then pan -0.9→+0.9 + ≤0.5 ms ITD

# IMPACT (sub dry+mono; everything else through reverb RT60 4.5 s)
def impact(dur=4.5, metal_f=310):
    t=tt(dur); n=len(t); r=np.random.randn
    click=S.sosfilt(S.butter(2,2500,'highpass',fs=SR,output='sos'),r(n))*np.exp(-t/.004)*.9
    body=sat(sine(48+122*np.exp(-t/.035))*np.exp(-t/.22),3)
    sub=sine(28+30*np.exp(-t/.45))*np.clip(t/.003,0,1)*np.exp(-t/.9)
    crack=sat(S.sosfilt(S.butter(2,[900,4500],'bandpass',fs=SR,output='sos'),r(n))*np.exp(-t/.07),4)*.5
    metal=.25*sum(np.sin(2*np.pi*metal_f*q*t)*np.exp(-t/d)/(k+1) for k,(q,d) in enumerate(zip([1,2.32,4.25,6.63,9.38],[1.3,.9,.6,.45,.3])))
    knock=S.sosfilt(S.butter(2,[100,220],'bandpass',fs=SR,output='sos'),r(n))*np.exp(-t/.05)*.8
    return reverb(click+body+crack+metal+knock, 4.5, -3) + sub    # sub added after reverb

# BRAAM: see the technique entry; core loop:
#  for mult,nv,spread,g in [(1,7,18,1),(2,5,14,.55),(3,3,10,.25)]: sum nv polyblep_saws at f0*mult*2**((pitch+cents/100)/12)
#  pitch = -0.6*exp(-t/.04) + fall*clip((p-.5)/.5,0,1)**2 ; x*=1+.22*sin(2π·31t) ; sat(x,3.5,.1)
#  LPF Q1.1: 150→2600 Hz in 80 ms then 750+1850*exp(-(t-.08)/.9) ; x += .9*sat(bandpass420-1400(x),2.5)
#  + .35*sat(sine(f0 pitch-env),1.6) + breath ; amp attack 45 ms, release last 45% ; reverb RT60 5 s -6 dB

# RISSET RHYTHM (forever-accelerating ticks)
#  for k in range(3): pos=(k+rate*t)%3; bpm=60*2**pos; beat=np.cumsum(bpm/60/SR); fire where floor(beat) increments; gain .5-.5cos(2π pos/3)

# GLITCH: stutter = tile(slice*fade2ms); roll slices 1/16→1/32→1/64 note; bitcrush 6 bit + hold 10; tape_stop rate=linspace(1,0)^1.6, y=interp(cumsum(rate), idx, x)

# TRUE-PEAK LIMITER + MASTER
from scipy.ndimage import maximum_filter1d
def limiter(x, ceil_db=-1.2, look_ms=5, rel_ms=150):
    c=db(ceil_db); up=S.resample_poly(x,4,1,axis=1)
    pk=np.abs(up).max(0)[:4*x.shape[1]].reshape(-1,4).max(1)        # 4x true-peak detector
    la=int(look_ms*SR/1000); g=np.minimum(1,c/(maximum_filter1d(pk,2*la+1)+1e-12))
    a=np.exp(-1/(rel_ms*SR/1000)); gs=np.minimum(S.lfilter([1-a],[1,-a],g),g)
    gs=np.minimum(S.lfilter([1-a],[1,-a],gs[::-1])[::-1],g); return x*gs
def master(mix):   # tested: -14.3 LUFS-I / -1.2 dBTP / LRA 9.9 on the 40 s demo
    y=S.sosfilt(S.butter(4,28,'highpass',fs=SR,output='sos'),mix,axis=-1)
    y=np.tanh(y*db(8)*1.2)/1.2
    return limiter(y,-1.2,5,150)
# verify: ffmpeg -i mix.wav -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -   (I, LRA, True peak)

=== E. RENDER ORDER ===
1) timeline.json (beats → frames → samples). 2) Render each one-shot once (fixed seeds) and cache it. 3) Place onto MUSIC / SFX / HITS buses (place() at onset; place_end() for risers and reverse swells). 4) Sidechain MUSIC+SFX from HITS envelope. 5) Gate all buses to zero inside air pockets. 6) Sum 0.5*(music+sfx) + 0.5*hits. 7) master(). 8) Measure, then mux with ffmpeg -c:a aac -b:a 320k.

### Pitfalls
- Fading a riser out instead of hard-cutting it, or letting its peak miss the hit frame. A riser that dies before the hit, or overshoots it, kills the release. Use place_end() so it ends sample-exactly 1-8 frames before the hit.
- 'Silence' that isn't silent. Reverb tails from earlier hits leak into the air pocket and turn it to mush. Gate every bus, tails included, to digital zero for the gap (the test spectrogram shows a clean black band only because of this).
- Constant loudness. If Act I is not around -24 to -18 LUFS-S, nothing later can feel big, and wall-to-wall energy is exactly the 'no hits land' failure IRPR warns about.
- Overusing the braam or the ticking clock. Both are clichés (braam saturation by 2014; the tick went out from overuse). More than 6 braams, or braams closer than 4 bars, reads as parody.
- Sub that only exists below 60 Hz. It is inaudible on laptops and phones and eats limiter headroom while K-weighted LUFS barely counts it. The first braam prototype had 92% of its energy below 120 Hz. Add saturation harmonics, a 100-220 Hz knock and a 420-1400 Hz rasp, and HPF the master at 28 Hz.
- Non-band-limited oscillators. A naive saw aliases on high notes, and Shepard partials above Nyquist folded back as audible descending lines in the first prototype. Use polyBLEP and mask partials above 0.45·SR.
- Using a sample-peak limiter as a true-peak limiter. A -1.5 dBFS sample-peak ceiling still measured 0.0 dBTP after saturation. Detect peaks on a 4× oversampled signal and leave a ceiling of at least -1 dBTP.
- Mastering hot (-8 to -10 LUFS) 'to compete'. Web platforms normalize loud content down (the rule of thumb is -14 LUFS), so you only lose transient punch. Keep LRA at 8-12 LU.
- Clicks from abrupt pitch or filter changes. Always integrate phase (cumsum of frequency), update filter coefficients every ≤32 samples with carried state, keep Q ≤ 12 on fast sweeps, and give every buffer-repeat slice a 1-2 ms fade.
- Too many layers in one band. Stacking riser + strings + Shepard + whoosh all at 1-4 kHz turns into noise, and excess 2-8 kHz is fatiguing (and penalized by Leq(m) theatrically). Give each element its slot and mute what doesn't earn its place.
- Putting reverb on the sub or on the whole hit. Sub tails go to mud and muddy the next downbeat. Keep sub dry and mono, and HPF reverb returns at about 150 Hz.
- Unequal or drifting AV sync. Audio placed at float times rounded differently from the video frames drifts by up to a frame. Derive every onset from integer frames (×1600 samples), and never let audio lead picture by more than about 1 frame (BT.1359 detectability: about 45 ms).
- No motif. Pure sound design with no 3-5 note idea feels generic. Even sound-design-led cues need 'always melody'.
- Over-dense builds. If the build already has hits on every beat, the hit run has nowhere to go. Keep build hits sparse (one per bar at most) until the final 2 bars.
- Non-deterministic renders (unseeded RNG). Each re-render then sounds slightly different and breaks review-to-final parity. Seed every generator.
- Python per-sample loops on 150 s of audio are very slow. Use vectorized numpy plus block-wise lfilter (the whole 40 s demo rendered in 5.5 s on 4 cores).
- Muted autoplay. On social feeds many viewers start muted, so the kinetic typography must carry the story alone. Sound design amplifies it but cannot be the only carrier of meaning.
